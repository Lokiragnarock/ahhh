## 2. Course objectives and outcomes

Course description: introductory course on sustainable finance covering how ESG factors shape investment decisions for corporate managers, professional investors, and market participants; how to interpret ESG metrics against financial returns; risk measurement for issues like climate change; basics of impact investing and sustainable/responsible investment aligned with the UN SDGs.

Objectives:
1. Understand the fundamental concepts of sustainable finance and its significance in financial decision-making.
2. Describe the basics of ESG factors and their applications in the finance domain.
3. Analyse the impact of recent policy and regulatory changes on sustainable finance practices.
4. Evaluate various sustainable finance products and instruments in financial markets.
5. Examine how sustainability issues influence management decisions through real-world case studies.

CLOs:
- **CLO1**: Understand the fundamental concepts of sustainable finance.
- **CLO2**: Describe the basics of ESG factors and their applications in the finance domain.
- **CLO3**: Analyse the impact of recent policy and regulatory changes on sustainable finance.
- **CLO4**: Evaluate sustainable finance products and instruments.
- **CLO5**: Examine how sustainability issues affect management decisions through case studies.

Domain skill: ability to explore, evaluate, and interpret the implications of sustainable finance practices in corporate operations and strategic decision-making. SDG Goal: SDG-8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth, per the PDF, though the field itself contains a typo: "Decent week and Economic Growth").

## 5. CIA details

### CIA-I — Inside the ESG Reality of a Company: Beyond Greenwashing (ESG Intelligence Profile)
Individual. Each student prepares an ESG Intelligence Profile on one assigned/approved publicly listed company using annual reports, BRSR/sustainability reports, ESG databases, company websites, media reports. Must:
- Map the company's ESG risk profile across Environmental, Social, and Governance dimensions.
- Benchmark against at least **two industry peers** using ESG indicators.
- Conduct relative ESG analysis (strengths, weaknesses, sustainability positioning).
- Connect company initiatives to relevant UN SDGs.
- Identify disclosure gaps, ESG controversies, and possible greenwashing tendencies.
- Propose practical ESG/sustainable finance improvement strategies.
- Conduct a short stakeholder perception survey/interview (**minimum 10 respondents**) on the company's sustainability/ESG initiatives.

Phased submission structure:
| Phase | Date | Component |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13 July 2026 | Company selection & ESG source identification |
| 2 | 14 July 2026 | ESG risk matrix preparation |
| 3 | 15 July 2026 | Peer benchmarking & relative ESG analysis |
| 4 | 22 July 2026 | Stakeholder survey & interpretation |
| 5 | 24 July 2026 | ESG disclosure gap analysis & greenwashing evaluation |
| 6 | 25 July 2026 | ESG improvement strategy |
| Final | **26 July 2026** | ESG report + poster/infographic |

Report requirements: company snapshot & ESG reputation; ESG risk matrix (environmental — carbon emissions, water usage, waste management, energy dependency; social — labour welfare, diversity & inclusion, customer privacy, community relations; governance — board independence, executive compensation, ethical controversies, transparency); peer benchmarking & relative ESG analysis (ESG ratings, net zero targets, women on board, renewable energy usage, sustainability disclosures, governance practices — assessing which company performs better and why, strongest/weakest pillar, proactive vs reactive, long-term value); SDG alignment mapping (most genuinely addressed SDG, most ignored/weakly addressed SDG); ESG disclosure gap analysis (measurable targets, improving performance, vague claims, greenwashing evidence, media controversies — must identify "one major ESG promise versus actual reported outcome"); ESG improvement strategy (environmental, social, governance, reporting/transparency, sustainable finance initiatives); stakeholder perception survey & interpretation; one-page ESG insight poster/infographic (ESG score snapshot, key ESG risks, SDG alignment, green flags vs red flags, final ESG rating/recommendation).

Submission components: ESG Intelligence Profile Report, ESG Risk Matrix, Peer Benchmarking Table, SDG Mapping & Gap Analysis, ESG Improvement Strategy, Survey Findings & Interpretation, one-page poster/infographic, survey evidence/screenshots, references in APA format, **AI tools and prompts used disclosed**, reflection note on modifications/originality improvements made to AI-generated outputs.

Academic integrity note: AI tools are for support only (idea generation, grammar, data organization) — benchmarking interpretation, stakeholder analysis, survey findings, strategic recommendations, and critical evaluation must reflect original student understanding.

Submission mode: Google Classroom. Individual. **Deadline: 26/07/2026.**
Rubric top band (4 criteria, 20 marks converted to 15): comprehensive and insightful mapping of E/S/G risks with strong evidence and interpretation; excellent comparison with peers using multiple credible ESG sources and meaningful interpretation; strong connection between company practices and SDGs with critical insight; highly professional, visually engaging, well-structured, persuasive presentation.

### CIA 3 — Sustainable Finance War Room: ESG Crisis & Strategic Response Simulation
Individual. Assume the role of a Sustainable Finance Strategy Consultant advising a government, financial institution, sovereign wealth fund, stock exchange, or multinational corporation facing a sustainability-related financial challenge. Choose one country-specific global initiative (see table below or an approved equivalent) and design a sustainable finance strategy. Delivered as a simulated boardroom-style policy advisory presentation to classmates acting as investors/regulators/stakeholders, who **peer-evaluate as 20% of the assignment weightage**.

| Country | Global initiative / focus |
|---|---|
| India | Sovereign Green Bonds & SEBI ESG Disclosure Framework |
| European Union | EU Green Deal & EU Taxonomy |
| United States | Inflation Reduction Act & Climate Investment Incentives |
| China | Green Credit Policy & Carbon Neutrality 2060 |
| Japan | GX (Green Transformation) Financing Strategy |
| United Kingdom | Transition Finance Framework |
| Singapore | MAS Green Finance Action Plan |
| Brazil | Amazon Conservation & Climate Finance |
| UAE | Sustainable Finance Framework & Net Zero 2050 |
| South Africa | Just Energy Transition Investment Plan |
| Indonesia | Blue Bonds & Energy Transition Mechanism |
| Kenya | Climate Adaptation Finance & Green Bond Program |

Must prepare and present: (1) country & ESG context analysis (sustainability challenge, ESG/climate finance context, stakeholder concerns, economic/regulatory background); (2) global initiative evaluation (objectives, significance, ESG relevance, opportunities/challenges); (3) sustainable finance strategy design using **at least two tools** from green bonds, transition finance, sustainability-linked finance, ESG investments, carbon markets, climate finance mechanisms, public-private partnerships; (4) strategic trade-off & risk analysis (growth vs sustainability, profitability vs ESG commitments, transition risks, stakeholder conflicts, governance/implementation challenges); (5) financial & strategic recommendations (ESG improvement roadmap, SDG alignment, governance/disclosure recommendations, long-term value creation); (6) professional presentation with data visualization, ESG metrics, global benchmarks, policy comparisons, financial rationale.

Format: PPT (10–12 slides), 8–10 minute professional presentation, APA references, disclosed AI tool use and prompts, one/two-page reflection (learning outcomes, strategic insights, sustainability trade-offs, use of AI).

Submission mode: Google Classroom and in-class activity. Individual. **Deadline: 20 September 2026.**
Rubric top band (4-point scale across 5 criteria): strong sustainability/policy analysis with relevant financial insights; well-developed, financially viable, strategically aligned strategy; strong application of international sustainability frameworks and SDGs; critical and insightful evaluation of trade-offs; professional presentation with strong visuals, clarity, and persuasive delivery.

## 7. Watch-outs

- **CIA-I has six hard phase deadlines (13, 14, 15, 22, 24, 25 July) before the final 26 July 2026 submission** — this is not a single-deadline assignment; missing an early phase likely costs marks on the phased-submission evaluation even if the final report is complete.
- Minimum **10 stakeholder survey respondents** required — start recruiting early given the tight July phase schedule.
- Must benchmark against **at least two industry peers** and identify **one specific ESG promise-vs-outcome gap** — both are explicit deliverables graders will look for by name.
- AI disclosure is mandatory and explicitly graded: tools used, prompts used, and a reflection on modifications made to AI output for originality — treat this as a required section, not optional boilerplate.
- CIA 3 has a **20% peer-evaluation weightage** — classmates grade the live presentation, so the boardroom-style delivery matters as much as the written strategy.
- CIA 3 requires **at least two named sustainable finance tools** applied to the chosen country's initiative — a strategy using only one tool undercuts that rubric criterion.
