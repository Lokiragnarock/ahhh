# Global Business Finance: Map of Content

## What this is
Entry point for Global Business Finance (BBA303F-6, BBA Semester VI Finance, faculty Dr. Ramesh Chandra Babu T). The notes are built from the course plan and from the essential text, Vij (2022), Taxmann's International Financial Management, with Madura and Eun and Resnick as backup for method. India-first examples (INR, RBI). **No faculty slides were available when these were written.** If class notes define a formula or step order differently, write the class version in the exam and treat these notes as the textbook route. Update this page when slides arrive.

**Scope now: Units 1 to 3 only**, which is what the mid-semester exam covers. **Units 4 and 5 are not written yet** (FOREX exposure and risk management, 12 h; international project appraisal, 9 h). Add them after the mid-sem.

## Open threads and [VERIFY] items
- **Faculty version.** Whether the faculty uses bid-based or ask-based spread percentage, the exact form of PPP and Fisher formulas, and the BoP layout (BPM6 as the RBI reports, or the older four-part layout in Vij). Notes use the textbook route.
- **IF-1.** Infosys ADR dates and amount (NASDAQ 1999, NYSE move 2012); Tata Motors and JLR acquisition year (2008).
- **IF-2.** Gold standard dates; mint prices ($20.67 per ounce and £4.2474); Latin Monetary Union ratio; Britain leaving gold in 1931; Bretton Woods attendance (44) and the ±1% band; Smithsonian (Dec 1971, ±2.25%); Jamaica Agreement (1976, effective 1978); India's 1991 and 1993 changes.
- **IF-3.** Current IMF AREAER classification (ten categories) and India's current label; EMS dates (1979 launch, 16 Sept 1992, ±15% in Aug 1993, euro 1999); Mexican peso crisis dates (20 and 22 Dec 1994), deficit level (about 7% of GDP) and rescue size (about US$50 billion).
- **IF-4.** Whether Vij places India at a particular corner of the trilemma.
- **BP-1 and BP-2.** When the RBI adopted the BPM6 format and its release lag; the RBI's published table layout (capital and financial account combined, reserve change sign).
- **BP-3 and BP-4.** India's current account deficit and remittance rank, 1990-91 deficit and reserves; benchmark ratios used in the Country X case (CAD 2 to 3% of GDP, import cover 3 to 6 months).
- **FX-1.** Latest BIS Triennial Survey turnover and USD share; role of CCIL and FBIL; money changer licensing.
- **FX-2.** Indian quoting conventions (yen per 100, FEDAI/FBIL) and spot, cash and tom settlement; T+1 for USD/CAD.
- **FX-4.** FEMA 1999 effective date, forward cover rules (underlying exposure, tenor, cancellation), FEDAI functions, the foreign exchange derivative regulations as currently in force, LRS limit (USD 2,50,000).
- **FX-5 and FX-6.** MIFOR definition; NEER and REER indices; the Big Mac index details.
- **Illustrative data.** Every rate, price and inflation figure in the worked examples is illustrative, not market data.

## Recall prompts
- Q: Exporter receives USD 8,00,000 in six months with a forward of 89.40 / 89.50. Which rate and what receipt?
- Q: Forward of 90.00 against a parity of 90.5385. Which four legs do you trade and what is the profit per USD 10,00,000?
- Q: Spot 88, inflation India 5% and US 3%. Expected spot after one year, exact and approximate?
- Q: Draw the BPM6 structure and say where dividends, remittances and FDI sit.
- Q: A country has a 4.75% of GDP current account deficit, 56% volatile financing and 3 months of import cover. Proceed, postpone or modify?
- Q: Compare fixed and flexible regimes and name the trilemma corners.
