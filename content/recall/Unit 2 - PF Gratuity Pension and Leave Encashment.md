# Unit 2 — PF, Profit in Lieu of Salary, Gratuity, Pension & Leave Encashment

Covers: treatment of provident fund, profit in lieu of salary, leave encashment (theory), retirement benefits (gratuity/pension/commuted pension — problems). Companion to [[Unit 2 - Perquisites]].

## Provident Fund — three types, three treatments

| Type                                                      | Employee's contribution                      | Employer's contribution                                                  | Interest                                                                                                                                                                                           | Lump sum at retirement/resignation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Statutory PF (SPF)** — govt/semi-govt/university staff  | 80C-eligible deduction                       | Fully exempt                                                             | Fully exempt (but interest on contributions over ₹2.5 lakh/year taxable, effective 1 Apr 2021)                                                                                                     | Fully exempt                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Recognised PF (RPF)** — private sector, approved by CIT | 80C-eligible deduction                       | Exempt up to **12% of salary**; excess is taxable as salary (perquisite) | Exempt up to 9.5% p.a. (or as prescribed); interest on employee contribution exempt only up to ₹2,50,000/year contribution (₹5,00,000 if employer makes no contribution) — excess interest taxable | Exempt, subject to minimum service conditions (5 years continuous service, or specified exceptions like ill-health/employer closure)                                                                                                                                 |
| **Unrecognised PF (URPF)** — not CIT-approved             | No 80C deduction                             | Not taxable *in the year contributed*                                    | Not taxable in the year credited                                                                                                                                                                   | On withdrawal: employer's contribution + interest thereon is taxed as "Profit in lieu of salary"; interest on employee's own contribution taxed as "Income from Other Sources"; employee's own principal contribution is not taxed again (already taxed when earned) |
| **Public Provident Fund (PPF)**                           | 80C-eligible deduction (₹500–₹1,50,000/year) | N/A (self-contribution scheme)                                           | Fully exempt                                                                                                                                                                                       | Fully exempt (after 15-year lock-in)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

**Recall prompt:** Employer contributes 15% of salary to an employee's Recognised PF. What happens to the excess 3%? → It's taxable as a perquisite (salary income) in the employee's hands, since only 12% of salary is exempt for employer RPF contributions.

## Profit in lieu of salary (Section 17(3))

Payments received by an employee **as compensation** connected with employment but not strictly "salary" — e.g., compensation on termination/modification of employment terms, payment from an unrecognised PF/superannuation fund (employer's share + interest), any sum received under a Keyman Insurance Policy, any amount received before joining or after cessation of employment (e.g., a signing bonus tied to employment). Taxed as salary income even though it isn't a periodic wage.

**Recall prompt:** An employee receives ₹5 lakh as compensation for early termination of contract. Which head of income? → "Profit in lieu of salary" under Section 17(3) — taxed as Salary, not Capital Gains or Other Sources, because it arises from the employment relationship.

## Leave encashment — Section 10(10AA) (theory)

- **During service**: leave encashment while still employed is **fully taxable** (with relief available under Section 89 for the "bunching" effect on tax rate).
- **At retirement/resignation**:
  - **Government employees**: fully exempt.
  - **Non-government employees**: exempt to the **least of**:
    (a) Actual leave encashment received
    (b) 10 months' average salary
    (c) Cash equivalent of unutilised leave (max 30 days per year of service)
    (d) A statutory monetary ceiling notified by the government (this ceiling has been revised upward in recent years — verify the current figure before using it in a numeric answer)
  Balance beyond the least of these is taxable.

**Recall prompt:** Why does the government-employee case get simpler treatment than the private-sector case? → Government leave-encashment rules are a direct statutory exemption (10(10AA)(i)), while private-sector employees fall under the "least of four limits" formula (10(10AA)(ii)) — a policy distinction long-tested as a straight recall MCQ.

## Retirement benefits — Gratuity (Section 10(10))

Two categories:

**1. Government employees** — gratuity **fully exempt**, no ceiling.

**2. Employees covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972** (private sector, most establishments with 10+ employees):
Exempt = **least of**:
(a) Actual gratuity received
(b) **15 days' salary** (last drawn) **× number of years of service** (part of a year ≥ 6 months rounds up to a full year); 15 days computed as salary ÷ 26 × 15
(c) Statutory ceiling (₹20,00,000, as last revised — confirm current figure)

**3. Employees NOT covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act** (private sector, smaller establishments):
Exempt = least of:
(a) Actual gratuity received
(b) **Half month's average salary** (avg of last 10 months) **× completed years of service** (no rounding for part years)
(c) ₹20,00,000 statutory ceiling

**Recall prompt:** Why does "15 days ÷ 26" appear in the Payment-of-Gratuity-Act formula? → 26 working days/month is the standard divisor under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 (excludes weekly offs) — distinguishing it from the "half month of a 30-day month" logic used for non-covered employees.

## Pension and commuted pension (Section 10(10A))

- **Uncommuted (regular monthly) pension**: fully taxable for all employees (govt and non-govt alike).
- **Commuted pension** (lump sum taken in lieu of part of future monthly pension):
  - **Government employees**: fully exempt.
  - **Non-government employees**:
    - If **gratuity is also received**: exempt to the extent of **1/3rd** of the full value of the pension that would've been received if 100% commuted.
    - If **no gratuity is received**: exempt to the extent of **1/2 (one-half)** of that full value.
  Balance is taxable.

**Recall prompt:** A private-sector employee commutes 50% of pension and also receives gratuity. Full commutation value (i.e., value of 100% commutation) works out to ₹6,00,000. What's exempt? → 1/3rd of ₹6,00,000 = ₹2,00,000 exempt (since gratuity was also received) — note this is 1/3rd of the *full* (100%) commutation value, not 1/3rd of the amount actually received for the 50% commuted.
