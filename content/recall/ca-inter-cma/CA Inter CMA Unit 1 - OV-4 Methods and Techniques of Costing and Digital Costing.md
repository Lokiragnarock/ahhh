# OV-4: Methods and Techniques of Costing, and Digital Costing

Covers: the seven methods of costing and the industries that use them, the techniques of costing, the impact of IT and ERP on cost accounting, and the digital costing system with its benefits. **(ICAI SM ch. 1, paras 9, 14, 15)**

## Where this fits in the exam

MCQs ("process costing is suitable for...") and the descriptive question "STATE the method of costing and the suggested cost unit for the following industries". Digital costing is new in the syllabus, so expect short theory parts on it.

## Methods vs techniques

**Method** = how cost is collected for a type of output (job, process, service). **Technique** = how cost is analysed or presented (marginal, standard, absorption). A technique can sit on top of any method.

## Seven methods of costing

| Method | What is costed | Typical industry |
|---|---|---|
| Single or output (unit) costing | One product, produced continuously | Bricks, coal, cold drinks |
| Job costing | Each customer order separately | Printing press, motor workshop, advertising |
| Batch costing (extension of job costing) | A batch of identical items; cost per unit = batch cost ÷ units | Pharmaceuticals, components |
| Contract costing | Each contract | Bridges, roads, buildings, real estate |
| Process costing (operation costing for mechanical operations) | Each stage or process | Sugar, paper (pulp, then paper), oil refining |
| Operating (service) costing | Services | Transport, water supply, hospitals |
| Multiple costing | A combination | Bicycles, car assembly (parts by job/batch, assembly by output costing) |

**Activity Based Costing** assigns costs to activities first, then to cost objects using each activity's cost driver (EL-11).

## Techniques of costing

| Technique | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Uniform costing | Firms in an industry agree to the same system and terminology; allows inter-firm comparison and an industry cost (useful when seeking tax relief or protection) |
| Marginal costing | Separates fixed and variable cost; shows the effect of volume or product-mix changes on profit (CC-5) |
| Standard costing and variance analysis | Pre-determine costs, compare with actual; best for standardised, repetitive production (CC-1) |
| Historical costing | Cost after it is incurred: post costing (after completion) or continuous costing (as soon as, or while, a job is done); limited use |
| Absorption costing | Charges all costs, fixed and variable, to products (contrast with marginal costing) |

## Impact of IT and ERP

1. ERP integrates functions: one entry gives custom reports; cost and financial reconciliation becomes simpler.
2. Paperless working: bill of materials, material requisition, goods received notes as e-copies.
3. Internet, intranet and extranet speed up procurement and support Just-in-Time.
4. Codification of cost centres and objects automates cost accumulation; reports job-wise, batch-wise, process-wise.
5. Uniform reports and budgets across locations, currencies and languages.
6. Real-time variance reports for immediate control.
7. Close monitoring of each process to remove non-value-added activities.

## Digital costing system

Collects, classifies and processes data like a conventional system, but differs in how data is collected, stored, analysed and reported. It links production, procurement and inventory with suppliers', customers' and market systems through data sharing.

**Data it provides:** cost incurred on a cost object; time spent; resource consumption; current market prices of product and raw materials; lead time and availability of materials; product demand and trend.

**Benefits (using AI, machine learning and big data):**
1. Cost ascertained with certainty for each cost object (better allocation and apportionment).
2. Time-spent data for designing incentive plans.
3. Material requirement planning; zero-wastage and JIT possibilities.
4. Identifying and removing non-value-added activities.
5. Standards set and variances measured in real time.
6. Estimates and standards on a marked-to-market (M2M) basis.
7. Demand prediction from customer behaviour, for budgets and production planning.
8. Better cost-behaviour analysis for informed decisions.

## Traps

- Calling batch costing an extension of process costing. ICAI: it is an extension of **job** costing.
- Calling marginal costing a method. It is a **technique**.
- Operation costing is process costing applied to mechanical operations, not a service method.

## What to remember

- Methods: unit, job, batch, contract, process, operating, multiple.
- Techniques: uniform, marginal, standard, historical, absorption.
- Digital costing: six data types, eight benefits; M2M standards and real-time variances are the distinctive ones.

## Flashcards
Q: Batch costing is an extension of which method?
A: Job costing.

Q: Which method suits a sugar mill?
A: Process costing.

Q: Which method suits a hospital or transport undertaking?
A: Operating (service) costing.

Q: What is multiple costing? Example?
A: A combination of two or more methods, for example bicycles (parts by job or batch costing, assembly by output costing).

Q: Two advantages of uniform costing?
A: Inter-firm comparison with other firms or the industry average; an industry-wide cost of production, useful when seeking tax relief or protection.

Q: Difference between post costing and continuous costing?
A: Post costing ascertains cost after production is complete; continuous costing ascertains it as soon as, or while, the job is in progress.

Q: Name four kinds of data a digital costing system provides.
A: Any four of: cost incurred on a cost object, time spent, resource consumption, current market prices, lead time and availability of materials, demand and trend.
