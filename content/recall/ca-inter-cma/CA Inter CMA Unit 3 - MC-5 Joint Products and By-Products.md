# MC-5: Joint Products and By-Products

Covers: joint products, by-products and co-products; split-off point; joint costs; every ICAI method of apportioning joint costs (physical units, NRV at split-off, technical estimates, market value at separation, market value after further processing, average unit cost, contribution margin) with when to use each; the sell-or-process-further decision; by-product valuation methods and treatment; with ICAI Examples 1-3 and Illustrations 1-4. **(ICAI SM ch. 11)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Apportioning joint cost by two or three methods, plus a further-processing decision (Ill. 4), is a regular 5-10 mark question; definitions and "which method when" are MCQs.

## Definitions

- **Joint products:** two or more products separated in the same processing operation, usually needing further processing, none of which is a major product: equal importance, each with significant sale value (petrol, fuel oil, lubricants, paraffin, kerosene from crude).
- **By-products:** recovered from material discarded in a main process, or incidentally produced, with a value **relatively insignificant** compared with the main product (molasses from sugar; tar, ammonia and benzole from coal; glycerine from soap).
- **Co-products:** contemporary products that do **not** necessarily come from the same material or process (wheat and gram from separate farms; timber boards from different trees).
- **Split-off point:** where joint products become separately identifiable. Costs up to it are **joint costs**; costs after it are product-specific.

Joint vs by-products: equal importance vs small value; produced simultaneously vs incidentally.

Apportionment matters for (a) closing inventory valuation, (b) pricing, (c) profit per product.

## Methods of apportioning joint costs

| Method | Basis | ICAI: use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical units** | Weight, number at split-off (loss shared on the same basis) | Selling prices are uniform; defect: treats all products as equal |
| **NRV at split-off** | Final sales − post split-off costs (and selling costs, profit margin where given) | Widely used; realisable value at split-off not known |
| **Technical estimates** | Engineers' estimates of resources used | Other results do not match resource use, or values unavailable |
| Market value at separation | Sales value at split-off (multiplying factor = joint cost ÷ total sales value × 100) | Split-off prices known; further processing costs disproportionate |
| Market value after further processing | Final sales value | Further processing costs proportionate and all products processed further |
| Average unit cost | Joint cost ÷ total units (same as physical units) | Units are in the same measure |
| Contribution margin | Variable joint cost by physical units; fixed joint cost by contribution | When fixed and variable joint costs are separated |

### ICAI Illustration 1: physical units with wastage

5,000 tonnes of coal at ₹1,100 = ₹55,00,000. Output: coke 3,500, tar 1,200, sulphate of ammonia 52, benzole 48 (4,800 tonnes; 200 wastage). Share wastage by output (200 ÷ 4,800): coke 146, tar 50, sulphate 2, benzole 2. Inputs 3,646 / 1,250 / 54 / 50 × ₹1,100 = **₹40,10,600 / ₹13,75,000 / ₹59,400 / ₹55,000**.

### ICAI Examples 1-3: the same joint cost three ways

Joint cost ₹64,500; A 200 units at ₹170, B 200 at ₹260 (sales ₹86,000); further processing A ₹4,000, B ₹32,000.

| Method | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| NRV (34,000 − 4,000 = 30,000 : 52,000 − 32,000 = 20,000 → 3:2) | 38,700 | 25,800 |
| Market value at separation (factor 64,500 ÷ 86,000 = 75%) | 25,500 | 39,000 |
| Market value after processing (A ₹200, B ₹300 → 40,000 : 60,000 = 2:3) | 25,800 | 38,700 |

Market value after processing is unfair when further processing costs are disproportionate (B's ₹32,000); NRV corrects this.

### ICAI Illustration 2: average unit cost

Joint cost ₹60,000; A 500, B 200, C 300 units. ₹60 per unit: A ₹30,000, B ₹12,000, C ₹18,000. Uniform unit cost; buyers of high-quality items benefit if prices follow it.

### ICAI Illustration 3: contribution margin method

A 100 kg at ₹60; B 120 kg at ₹30. Joint marginal cost ₹4,400; fixed ₹3,900.
1. Marginal cost by weight (100:120): A ₹2,000, B ₹2,400.
2. Contribution: A 6,000 − 2,000 = 4,000; B 3,600 − 2,400 = 1,200 (40:12).
3. Fixed cost by contribution: A 3,900 × 40/52 = **₹3,000**; B **₹900**.

## ICAI Illustration 4: three methods and the further-processing decision

Inorganic Chemicals: salt ₹40,000 + conversion ₹60,000 = joint cost ₹1,00,000. Caustic soda 1,200 t sold at ₹50; chlorine 800 t (market ₹75) all processed into 500 t of PVC at ₹200, incremental cost ₹20,000.

| Method | Caustic soda | Chlorine |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Sales value at split-off (60,000 : 60,000) | 50,000 | 50,000 |
| (b) Physical units (1,200 : 800) | 60,000 | 40,000 |
| (c) Estimated NRV (60,000 : 1,00,000 − 20,000 = 80,000 → 3:4) | 42,857 | 57,143 |

**Sell chlorine at ₹75 instead of making PVC?** Incremental revenue from processing = 500 × 200 − 800 × 75 = 1,00,000 − 60,000 = 40,000; incremental cost 20,000; **processing adds ₹20,000**. Accepting the offer would **reduce operating income by ₹20,000**.

**Decision rule:** joint costs are **irrelevant** to sell-or-process: process further only if incremental revenue > incremental cost.

## By-products

**Valuation methods:**
1. **Net realisable value:** by-product sales (less its further processing and its own selling and administration costs) deducted from the main product's cost (molasses reduces the cost of sugar). Keep separate accounts for those by-product expenses.
2. **Standard cost (technical estimates):** where the by-product is not saleable as it emerges or no comparable prices exist.
3. **Comparative price:** price of a similar or alternative material (blast furnace gas valued at what a gas company would charge).
4. **Re-use basis:** valued as the material it replaces in the process (or in an earlier process).

**Treatment in cost accounts:**
- **Small total value:** either credit sales to Costing P&L (miscellaneous income or extra sales), or deduct sale proceeds from production cost or cost of sales.
- **Considerable value:** treat as joint products and apportion joint costs (relative market values, physical output, or ultimate selling prices).
- **Needing further processing:** NRV at split-off = realisable value − further processing cost; then treat as small or considerable.

## Traps

- Including joint costs in the further-processing decision. Only incremental revenue and cost count.
- Using final sales values (market value after processing) when further processing costs differ widely: use NRV.
- Physical units method with wastage: share the wastage too (or use input quantities).
- Treating by-product revenue as a main product sale when its value is significant: then it is a joint product.

## What to remember

- Joint = equal importance; by-product = small value; co-products = different material or process.
- Ex 1-3: NRV A 38,700 / B 25,800; separation value A 25,500 / B 39,000; after processing A 25,800 / B 38,700.
- Ill. 3: fixed joint cost A ₹3,000, B ₹900. Ill. 4: NRV 42,857 / 57,143; processing PVC adds ₹20,000.
- By-products: NRV, standard cost, comparative price, re-use.

## Flashcards
Q: Define joint products.
A: Two or more products separated in the same processing operation, of equal importance, none designated as the major product.

Q: Define by-products.
A: Products recovered incidentally from a main process, with value relatively insignificant compared with the main product.

Q: What are co-products?
A: Contemporary products that do not necessarily emerge from the same material in the same process.

Q: When does ICAI recommend the physical units method?
A: When selling prices of all products are uniform.

Q: How is NRV at split-off computed?
A: Final sales value less post split-off processing costs (and directly attributable selling costs, and profit margin where given).

Q: ICAI Ex 1: NRV A ₹30,000, B ₹20,000, joint cost ₹64,500. Apportionment?
A: A ₹38,700; B ₹25,800.

Q: Are joint costs relevant to a sell-or-process-further decision?
A: No. Only incremental revenue and incremental cost matter.

Q: ICAI Ill. 4: effect of selling chlorine at ₹75 instead of making PVC?
A: Operating income falls by ₹20,000.

Q: Four methods of valuing by-products?
A: Net realisable value, standard cost (technical estimates), comparative price, re-use basis.
