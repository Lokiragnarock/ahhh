# EL-1: Material Procurement and Valuation of Receipts

Covers: objectives, requirements and elements of material control; the procurement procedure and its documents (bill of materials, material requisition note, purchase requisition, RFP or tender, purchase order, goods received note, material returned note); the five purchase questions including GeM; and ICAI's item-by-item table for valuing material receipts, with Illustrations 1 and 2. **(ICAI SM ch. 2, paras 1-4)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Valuation of receipts is a reliable MCQ (cash discount, GST with credit, demurrage, returnable containers) and a 4-5 mark computation (rate per kg after shortages). Bill of materials vs material requisition note is a standard theory part.

## Material control

**Objectives:** no interruption to production; optimise material cost (price, ordering and holding cost); reduce wastage; adequate information; complete orders on time.

**Requirements (ICAI list of ten):** co-ordination of all departments; a purchase procedure; standard forms; material budgets; internal check; proper storage; perpetual inventory with continuous stock checking; stores control and issue; control accounts and subsidiary records; regular reports.

**Elements:** procurement control, storage control, usage control, covering purchase, receipt, inspection, storage, issue, records and stock audit.

## Procurement procedure and documents

| Document | Prepared by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Bill of materials** (materials specification list) | Engineering or planning department | Standard quantities and qualities for a product or job; used by purchase, production, stores and costing |
| **Material requisition note** | Production (consuming) department | Authority to draw materials from stores |
| **Purchase requisition** | Store-keeper (regular items) or departmental head (special items) | Formal request to the purchase department to buy |
| **RFP / NIT / enquiry** | Purchase department | Invites quotations or tenders, offline or online (GeM) |
| **Comparative statement** | Purchase department | Compares price, quantity, quality, delivery, transport, payment terms, supplier reputation |
| **Purchase order** | Purchase manager | Written request to the supplier: materials, rates, period; copies to stores, receiving, costing |
| **Goods received note** | Receiving department | Quadruplicate: purchase, stores, receiving, accounts |
| **Material returned note** | Receiving / stores | For materials returned to supplier after they were entered in the receiving report |
| Invoice check | Accounts | Quantity checked against GRN, price against purchase order |

**Bill of materials vs material requisition note**

| Bill of materials | Material requisition note |
|---|---|
| Prepared by engineering or planning | Prepared by the consuming department |
| Complete schedule for a job | A request to issue materials |
| Can replace a requisition | Cannot replace a bill of materials |
| Can be used for quotations | Useful only for historical cost |
| Gives quantitative control over drawings | Shows what was actually drawn |

## The five purchase questions

1. **What?** As per requisitions; standard operating procedures for regular items.
2. **When?** Based on supply, lead time, consumption pattern (re-order level, JIT).
3. **How much?** Consumption, minimum order size, quantity discount, storage, working capital (EOQ).
4. **Where from?** Supplier selection must be transparent and fair, especially in PSUs (watched by CVC, CAG, auditors; the Constitution's "equality of opportunity"). Starts with an enquiry, RFP or NIT, local or global, offline or online.
5. **At what price?** Normally the lowest (L1) bidder who meets the criteria.

**Government e Marketplace (GeM):** a dedicated e-market for goods and services bought by government organisations, departments and PSUs; aims at transparency, efficiency and speed; provides e-bidding, reverse e-auction and demand aggregation; purchases through GeM are authorised and **made mandatory by the Ministry of Finance** for government users.

## Valuation of receipts (ICAI table)

Cost of material = purchase price net of trade discounts, rebates, duty drawback and input credit availed, plus costs of bringing it to its present location and condition.

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Trade discount, quantity discount | Deduct |
| **Cash discount** | **Ignore** (interest and finance item) |
| Subsidy, grant, incentive | Deduct |
| Road tax, toll paid by buyer | Add |
| **GST** | Exclude if credit is available (assume it is unless told otherwise) |
| Customs duty | Add (where credit is not allowed) |
| Demurrage, detention charges, fines, penalties | Exclude (abnormal) |
| Transit insurance, commission or brokerage, freight inwards | Add |
| Non-returnable containers | Add |
| Returnable containers | Exclude if fully refunded; add only the shortfall if the refund is less |
| Normal shortage (bulk breaking, evaporation) | Absorbed by good units (raises the rate) |
| Abnormal shortage (mishandling, pilferage) | Costing P&L Account |

## ICAI Illustration 1: rate per kg after shortage

Chemical A 10,000 kg at ₹10 = ₹1,00,000; chemical B 8,000 kg at ₹13 = ₹1,04,000; customs duty 10% (no credit) ₹20,400; railway freight ₹3,840. Normal breakage: A 500 kg, B 320 kg. Provision of 2% for further deterioration.

| | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Purchased (kg) | 10,000 | 8,000 |
| Less normal breakage | (500) | (320) |
| | 9,500 | 7,680 |
| Less 2% deterioration | (190) | (153.6) |
| **Effective quantity** | **9,310** | **7,526.4** |
| Price (₹) | 1,00,000 | 1,04,000 |
| Customs duty 10% | 10,000 | 10,400 |
| Freight (by quantity purchased, 5:4) | 2,133 | 1,707 |
| **Total cost** | **1,12,133** | **1,16,107** |
| **Rate per kg** | **₹12.04** | **₹15.43** |

Points: freight is shared in the ratio of **quantity purchased**, not value; the normal shortage and the deterioration provision both reduce the quantity, so good units carry the cost.

## ICAI Illustration 2: entry price in the stores ledger

200 units at ₹5 = ₹1,000, less 20% discount = ₹800; GST 12% ₹96 (credit available); packing in 5 non-returnable boxes ₹50; 2% cash discount if paid in 30 days.

Cost = 800 + 50 = ₹850; ÷ 200 = **₹4.25 per unit**. GST is out (credit available); cash discount is ignored (finance item); non-returnable packing is in.

## Traps

- Deducting cash discount. ICAI ignores it.
- Adding GST when nothing says credit is unavailable. ICAI: unless mentioned specifically, GST is not part of cost.
- Sharing freight by value. ICAI's illustration uses quantity purchased.
- Including demurrage. It is a penalty for delay: abnormal, excluded.

## What to remember

- Add: freight, insurance, customs (no credit), commission, non-returnable containers, toll. Deduct: trade and quantity discounts, subsidy. Ignore: cash discount. Exclude: GST with credit, demurrage, fines.
- Ill. 1: A ₹12.04, B ₹15.43 per kg. Ill. 2: ₹4.25 per unit.
- GeM is mandatory for government buyers (Ministry of Finance).

## Flashcards
Q: How is cash discount treated in valuing material receipts?
A: Ignored; it is an interest and finance item.

Q: Treatment of demurrage?
A: Abnormal cost, excluded from purchase cost.

Q: Returnable containers refunded at less than cost: treatment?
A: Only the shortfall is added to the cost of purchase.

Q: Who prepares the bill of materials? The material requisition note?
A: Engineering or planning department; the production (consuming) department.

Q: What is GeM?
A: Government e Marketplace: an e-market for government procurement with e-bidding, reverse e-auction and demand aggregation, made mandatory by the Ministry of Finance.

Q: In how many copies is a goods received note usually prepared, and for whom?
A: Four: purchase, stores (or indenting department), receiving and accounts.

Q: ICAI Ill. 2: invoice ₹800 after discount, GST ₹96 with credit, non-returnable boxes ₹50, 200 units. Rate?
A: ₹4.25 per unit.

Q: How does a normal shortage affect the rate per kg?
A: The good units absorb it, so the rate per kg rises.
