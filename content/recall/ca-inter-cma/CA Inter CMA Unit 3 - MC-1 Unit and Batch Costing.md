# MC-1: Unit Costing and Batch Costing

Covers: job-type vs continuous industries; unit (single or output) costing and its cost collection; treatment of spoiled and defective work; batch costing and its procedure; Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ); job vs batch costing; with ICAI Illustrations 1-7. **(ICAI SM ch. 8)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Unit costing is a cost sheet with a twist (semi-variable overheads, pricing on selling price); batch costing gives price-per-order questions; EBQ is a quick formula MCQ or 4-mark part. Unit 3 overall carries 20-25%.

## Two kinds of industry

- **Job work** (each order distinct, to customer specification): ship building, roads and bridges, heavy machinery, furniture. Methods: job, contract, batch costing.
- **Continuous or process** (uniform products, indistinguishable lots): chemicals, pharmaceuticals, paper, food, paints, rubber, textiles. Methods: process, single output, operating costing.

## Unit costing

Output is identical and each unit costs the same. **Cost per unit = total cost of production ÷ units produced.** Used in paper, cement, steel, mining, breweries. Costs are collected element-wise: materials from requisitions; labour from job time cards (indirect labour from payroll to standing orders); overheads under standing orders, apportioned and absorbed.

**Spoiled and defective work:** within the normal rate, rectification or loss is charged to the entire output; beyond normal, or from abnormal causes, to Costing P&L.

### ICAI Illustration 1

Materials ₹4,00,000; wages ₹2,40,000; 3,200 machine hours at ₹40; office overheads 10% of works cost; selling ₹20 per unit; 10,000 units made and sold at ₹120.

| | Per unit (₹) | Total (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Materials | 40.00 | 4,00,000 |
| Wages | 24.00 | 2,40,000 |
| Prime cost | 64.00 | 6,40,000 |
| Manufacturing OH (3,200 × 40) | 12.80 | 1,28,000 |
| Works cost | 76.80 | 7,68,000 |
| Office OH 10% | 7.68 | 76,800 |
| Cost of goods sold | 84.48 | 8,44,800 |
| Selling (₹20 × 10,000) | 20.00 | 2,00,000 |
| **Cost of sales** | **104.48** | **10,44,800** |
| **Profit** | **15.52** | **1,55,200** |

### ICAI Illustration 2: semi-variable overheads and margin on price

Atharva Pharmacare, capacity 3,000 units a week. Weeks: 1,200 units (material 9,000, wages 3,600, OH 31,000); 1,600 (12,000, 4,800, 33,000); 1,800 (13,500, 5,400, 34,000). Price for 2,000 units at 20% profit on **selling price**?

1. Material ₹7.50 and labour ₹3 per unit (constant per unit).
2. Factory OH is semi-variable (per unit falls from ₹25.83 to ₹18.89). Variable = (33,000 − 31,000) ÷ (1,600 − 1,200) = **₹5 per unit**; fixed = 33,000 − 8,000 = **₹25,000**.
3. At 2,000 units: OH = 10,000 + 25,000 = ₹35,000.
4. Total cost = 15,000 + 6,000 + 35,000 = ₹56,000 (₹28 per unit).
5. 20% on price = 25% on cost: profit ₹14,000; **price ₹35 per unit** (₹70,000).

## Batch costing

A form of specific-order costing where the cost object is a **batch** (a lot processed together). Batches arise for technical or economic reasons (one pen at a time is too costly; 10,000 of a design is cheap). Each batch carries minimum costs regardless of size: set-up, supervision.

**Procedure:** a number for each batch; material from requisitions; labour from time cards or job tickets; overheads on machine or labour hours; **cost per unit = batch cost ÷ units in the batch**.

### ICAI Illustration 3: price for an order

Arnav Confectioners bakes at most 50 at a time; order for 600 muffins = **12 batches**. Per batch: material ₹500, wages ₹50, oven set-up ₹150; production OH 20% of wages; S&D and admin 10% of production cost; profit 25% of sales (= ⅓ of cost).

| Per batch | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Material + wages + set-up | 700.00 |
| Production OH (20% of 50) | 10.00 |
| Production cost | 710.00 |
| S&D and admin (10%) | 71.00 |
| Total cost | 781.00 |
| Profit (⅓ of cost) | 260.33 |
| **Price per batch** | **1,041.33** |

Order price = 12 × 1,041.33 = **₹12,496**; **₹20.83 per muffin**.

### ICAI Illustration 4: monthly batches (summary)

200 pieces a month at ₹8; each month's batch is charged with its own material and wages, and overhead at that month's rate per labour hour (e.g. January 12,000 ÷ 4,800 = ₹2.50 × 240 h = ₹600). Totals for 1,230 pieces: cost ₹9,025 (₹7.34 per piece); profit ₹815; May made a loss of ₹30. On the contracted 1,200 pieces: sales 9,600 − cost (1,200 × 7.34) 8,808 = **profit ₹792**.

## Economic Batch Quantity (EBQ)

Larger batches mean fewer set-ups but more inventory held; smaller batches the reverse. **EBQ = the batch size where set-up cost + holding cost is minimum.**

**EBQ = √(2DS ÷ C)**: D = annual demand; S = set-up cost per batch; C = carrying cost per unit per year.

### ICAI Illustration 5

Monthly demand 500; set-up ₹60; manufacturing cost ₹20; interest 10%. EBQ = √(2 × 6,000 × 60 ÷ 2) = √3,60,000 = **600 units**.

### ICAI Illustration 6

48,000 bearings a year; holding ₹1 per bearing per month (₹12 a year); set-up ₹3,200.
1. EBQ = √(2 × 48,000 × 3,200 ÷ 12) = **5,060 units**.
2. Runs = 48,000 ÷ 5,060 = 9.49, so 10; interval = 365 ÷ 10 = **36.5 days**.
3. Minimum holding cost = 5,060 ÷ 2 × 12 = **₹30,360**.

### ICAI Illustration 7: table method vs formula

Paint: 50,000 litres a year; set-up ₹100; storage ₹50 per sq m a year, 250 litres per sq m, so C = ₹0.20 per litre.

| Batch (litres) | Set-up cost (50,000 ÷ batch × 100) | Holding (batch ÷ 2 × 0.20) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4,000 | 1,250 | 400 | 1,650 |
| 5,000 | 1,000 | 500 | 1,500 |
| 6,000 | 833 | 600 | 1,433 |
| **7,000** | **714** | **700** | **1,414** |
| 8,000 | 625 | 800 | 1,425 |
| 9,000 | 556 | 900 | 1,456 |
| 10,000 | 500 | 1,000 | 1,500 |

Lowest at **7,000 litres** (₹1,414). Formula: √(2 × 50,000 × 100 ÷ 0.20) = **7,071 litres**, close to the table result. (ICAI's working note rounds 7.14 set-ups up to 8 for ₹800; the table uses fractional set-ups.)

## Job vs batch costing

| Job costing | Batch costing |
|---|---|
| Non-standard, non-repetitive products to customer specification | Homogeneous products in lots, continuous flow |
| Cost per job | Cost per batch, then per unit |
| Each job unique | Units in a batch lack individuality |

## Traps

- Profit "20% on selling price" is 25% on cost (and 25% on price is ⅓ on cost).
- Scaling semi-variable overheads in proportion to output (Ill. 2). Split them first.
- Batch set-up cost is per **batch**, not per unit: count the batches.
- EBQ with a monthly holding cost: convert C to a yearly figure.

## What to remember

- Unit cost = total cost ÷ units. Ill. 1: ₹104.48 cost, ₹15.52 profit per unit. Ill. 2: price ₹35.
- Batch: 12 batches × ₹1,041.33 = ₹12,496 (₹20.83 each).
- EBQ = √(2DS ÷ C). Ill. 5: 600; Ill. 6: 5,060, every 36.5 days, holding ₹30,360; Ill. 7: 7,000 (table) or 7,071 (formula).

## Flashcards
Q: Formula for EBQ?
A: √(2DS ÷ C): D annual demand, S set-up cost per batch, C carrying cost per unit per year.

Q: ICAI Ill. 5: monthly demand 500, set-up ₹60, cost ₹20, interest 10%. EBQ?
A: 600 units.

Q: ICAI Ill. 6: optimum run and interval?
A: 5,060 units; 10 runs, every 36.5 days.

Q: 20% profit on selling price equals what percentage on cost?
A: 25% on cost.

Q: ICAI Ill. 3: 600 muffins in batches of 50. Price per muffin?
A: ₹20.83 (₹12,496 for the order).

Q: Name four industries using unit costing.
A: Any four of paper, cement, steel, mining, breweries.

Q: Batch costing is a variant of which method?
A: Job (specific order) costing.

Q: ICAI Ill. 2: factory OH ₹31,000 at 1,200 units and ₹33,000 at 1,600 units. Variable rate and fixed part?
A: ₹5 per unit; ₹25,000 fixed.
