# OV-5: Cost Sheet for Manufacturing and Service

Covers: the four cost heads (prime cost, cost of production, cost of goods sold, cost of sales), what goes in each, ICAI's specimen format, the treatment of abnormal costs, subsidies, penalties and interest, and three ICAI illustrations, including the long Arnav Ispat classification problem. **(ICAI SM ch. 6)** The service-sector cost sheet is in MC-6.

## Where this fits in the exam

Cost sheets are a dependable 5-10 mark descriptive question. The marks are in **classifying each item to the right head** and knowing what to leave out. Also tested as MCQs ("GST with input tax credit is...", "interest is shown...").

## The four heads

```
Direct material + Direct employee + Direct expenses        = PRIME COST
+ Factory overheads                                         = Gross works cost
+ Opening WIP − Closing WIP                                 = FACTORY (WORKS) COST
+ Quality control + R&D (process) + Admin (production)
− Credit for recoveries (scrap) + Primary packing           = COST OF PRODUCTION
+ Opening finished goods − Closing finished goods           = COST OF GOODS SOLD
+ Admin (general) + Selling + Secondary packing + Distribution
(+ Interest and finance charges, shown separately)          = COST OF SALES
```

## What goes where (ICAI lists)

**Direct material consumed** = opening stock + purchases − closing stock. Add freight inwards, insurance and costs directly attributable to procurement, and **duties and taxes only if input tax credit is not available**. Deduct trade discounts.

**Direct employee cost:** wages and salary, allowances and incentives, overtime, bonus or ex-gratia, **employer's PF and similar contributions**, other benefits (medical, leave with pay, subsidised food, LTC, retirement provisions).

**Direct expenses:** power and fuel, steam; **royalty** for production; hire of specific equipment; technical know-how fees; **amortised cost of moulds, patterns, patents**; product-specific design and software; job work charges.

**Factory overheads:** consumable stores and spares; depreciation, repairs and insurance of factory building and plant; lease rent of production assets; indirect production staff (supervisors); drawing and design office; insurance of raw material and WIP stock; amortised jigs and fixtures; service departments (tool room, engineering and maintenance, pollution control).

**Between factory cost and cost of production:** quality control; R&D for improving the process or product; administration **related to production**; less realisable value of scrap and waste; plus **primary** packing (needed to hold and preserve the product). By-products: deduct their net realisable value.

**After cost of goods sold:** general administration (office depreciation, directors' and administrative salaries, audit and legal fees, directors' sitting fees); selling (sales salaries, sales office, advertising, website); **secondary** packing (to store, transport, promote); distribution (delivery staff, transport, delivery vehicles).

## Special items (para 4.2)

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Abnormal cost (material and quantifiable), e.g. pandemic or sudden-lockdown costs | Excluded from cost |
| Subsidy, grant, incentive | Deducted from the cost object it relates to |
| Penalty, fine, damages, demurrage | Not part of cost |
| Interest and finance charges | Not part of cost of production; shown **separately within cost of sales** |

## ICAI Illustration 1: simple cost sheet with stock

Raw materials ₹1,80,000; direct wages ₹90,000; 10,000 machine hours at ₹8; administration (general) ₹35,000; selling ₹5 per unit sold; 4,000 units made, 3,600 sold at ₹125.

| Particulars | Total (₹) | Per unit (₹) |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials | 1,80,000 | 45.00 |
| Direct wages | 90,000 | 22.50 |
| **Prime cost** | 2,70,000 | 67.50 |
| Factory overheads (10,000 × ₹8) | 80,000 | 20.00 |
| **Cost of production** (4,000 units) | 3,50,000 | 87.50 |
| Less closing finished goods (400 × 87.50) | (35,000) | |
| **Cost of goods sold** (3,600 units) | 3,15,000 | 87.50 |
| Administration (general) | 35,000 | 9.72 |
| Selling (3,600 × ₹5) | 18,000 | 5.00 |
| **Cost of sales** | 3,68,000 | 102.22 |

Profit = 3,600 × 125 − 3,68,000 = 4,50,000 − 3,68,000 = **₹82,000**. Note that closing stock is valued at **cost of production per unit**, and selling overhead is charged only on units **sold**.

**Lessons:** a mould lasting two years' production is amortised (half this year); the three functional managers split three ways (production control → production admin, finance → general admin, sales → selling); vehicles used by directors are administration, delivery vehicles are distribution; packing for re-distribution is secondary packing, so it is distribution.

## Traps

- Adding GST to material cost when ITC is available. Exclude it.
- Valuing closing finished goods at cost of sales per unit. Use **cost of production** per unit.
- Charging selling overhead on units produced. Charge it on units **sold** when it is given per unit.
- Putting interest in cost of production. ICAI shows it separately within cost of sales.
- Including fines, penalties or abnormal pandemic costs. They are not cost.

## What to remember

- Four heads: prime cost → cost of production → cost of goods sold → cost of sales.
- WIP adjusts at factory cost; finished goods adjust at cost of goods sold.
- Primary packing = cost of production; secondary packing = selling or distribution.
- Ill. 1: cost of sales ₹3,68,000, profit ₹82,000. Ill. 2: profit ₹1,18,000. Ill. 3: cost of sales ₹11,35,03,300.

## Flashcards
Q: Where are opening and closing WIP adjusted in a cost sheet?
A: After gross works cost, to arrive at factory (works) cost.

Q: Where are opening and closing finished goods adjusted?
A: After cost of production, to arrive at cost of goods sold.

Q: GST paid on raw materials with input tax credit available: in cost or not?
A: Not in cost.

Q: Primary vs secondary packing?
A: Primary (needed to hold and preserve the product) is part of cost of production; secondary (to store, transport, promote) is a selling or distribution cost.

Q: How does ICAI treat interest and finance charges in a cost sheet?
A: Not in cost of production; shown separately as part of cost of sales.

Q: Moulds costing ₹8,96,000 with a life of two years' production: charge for the year?
A: ₹4,48,000 as a direct expense (amortised).

Q: Salary of the production control manager goes where?
A: Administration overhead related to production, added before cost of production.

Q: ICAI Ill. 1: what is the profit?
A: ₹82,000 (sales ₹4,50,000 − cost of sales ₹3,68,000).

Q: Is a fine or penalty part of cost?
A: No.
