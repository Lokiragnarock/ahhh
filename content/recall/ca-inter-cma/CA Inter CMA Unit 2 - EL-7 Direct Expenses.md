# EL-7: Direct Expenses

Covers: what counts as a direct expense, how it is measured (including sub-contracting), how it is treated (prime cost; amortising lump sums; immaterial amounts to overheads), with ICAI Illustration 16. **(ICAI SM ch. 3, para 12)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Small but frequent: an MCQ ("royalty on production is...") or a classification line inside a cost sheet or job cost. Links back to OV-5.

## Definition

Expenses **other than direct material and direct employee cost**, incurred to make a product or provide a service, and **directly traceable in an economically feasible manner** to a cost object.

**Examples (ICAI):** royalty paid or payable for production or service; hire charges for specific equipment; product- or service-specific design or drawing; product- or service-specific software; other expenses directly related to production. (Chapter 6 also lists power and fuel, technical know-how fees, amortised moulds, patterns and patents, and job work charges.)

## Measurement

- At **invoice or agreed price**, net of rebate or discount, **including** duties and taxes for which input credit is not available, commission and other directly attributable costs.
- **Sub-contracting (job work):** at the agreed price; if the principal supplies material to the job worker, add the value of that material and incidental expenses to the job charges.

## Treatment

- Part of **prime cost** of the product or service it traces to.
- **Lump-sum or one-time payment:** amortise over the estimated production volume or benefit (for example moulds lasting two years: OV-5).
- **Immaterial amounts:** may be treated as overheads.

## ICAI Illustration 16: Aditya Ltd., three jobs

| Item | Treatment | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product blueprint for job A ₹1,40,000 | Direct expense (product-specific design) | 1,40,000 | | |
| Hire of machinery for job B ₹40,000 | Direct expense (specific equipment) | | 40,000 | |
| One-time software licence for job C ₹50,000 | Direct expense (job-specific software) | | | 50,000 |
| Office and administration ₹3,00,000 | Overhead | | | |
| Office attendants' salary ₹50,000 | Administration overhead | | | |
| Marketing manager's salary ₹1,20,000 | Selling overhead | | | |
| **Direct expenses** | | **1,40,000** | **40,000** | **50,000** |

The test is traceability to one job: anything serving the business generally is an overhead.

## Traps

- Calling royalty on **sales** a direct expense of production. Royalty on production is direct; royalty on sales is a selling cost.
- Adding GST on a direct expense when credit is available. Include only taxes without credit.
- Charging a lump-sum licence or mould entirely to the first period's output. Amortise it.

## What to remember

- Direct expense = traceable, not material, not labour.
- Measure at net price plus non-creditable taxes; job work at agreed price plus materials supplied.
- Ill. 16: A ₹1,40,000; B ₹40,000; C ₹50,000.

## Flashcards
Q: Define direct expenses.
A: Expenses other than direct material and direct employee cost, incurred for a product or service and directly traceable to it in an economically feasible manner.

Q: Royalty paid on production: direct or indirect?
A: Direct expense.

Q: How are lump-sum direct expenses treated?
A: Amortised over the estimated production volume or benefit.

Q: Job work where the principal supplies material: how measured?
A: Job charges plus the value of materials supplied and incidental expenses.

Q: Hire charges for machinery used on one specific job?
A: Direct expense of that job.

Q: Salary of the marketing manager?
A: Selling overhead.
