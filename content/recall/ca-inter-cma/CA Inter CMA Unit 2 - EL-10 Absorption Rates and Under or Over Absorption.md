# EL-10: Absorption Rates, Under or Over Absorption, and Non-Factory Overheads

Covers: the six absorption methods and why material-based methods are weak; direct and comprehensive machine hour rates; normal, pre-determined, blanket and departmental rates; under- and over-absorption and the supplementary rate; administration overheads (three accounting methods, control); selling and distribution overheads (bases, control); capacity concepts and idle capacity cost; ICAI's treatment of special items (interest, depreciation, packing, fringe benefits, re-erection, bad debts, training, canteen, carriage, welfare, night shift, R&D); with ICAI Illustrations 6-10. **(ICAI SM ch. 4, paras 4.5-11)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Machine hour rate (Ill. 6-7 type) and under-absorption with a supplementary rate (Ill. 8 type) are classic 5-10 mark questions; the reverse calculation of overhead rates from selling prices (Ill. 9) is a favourite. Special items and capacity terms are MCQs.

## Six absorption methods

| Method | Rate | ICAI view |
|---|---|---|
| % of direct material | Overheads ÷ direct material × 100 | Unsuitable (only for material handling and stores overhead) |
| % of prime cost | Overheads ÷ prime cost × 100 | Unsuitable |
| % of direct labour cost | Overheads ÷ direct wages × 100 | Simple; time recognised indirectly; ignores machines and skill |
| Labour hour rate | Overheads ÷ direct labour hours | Fully recognises time; best where little machinery |
| **Machine hour rate** | Overheads ÷ machine hours | Best where machines are the main factor |
| Rate per unit of output | Overheads ÷ units | Simplest; for a single uniform product |

**Why material and prime cost bases fail:** overheads are mostly a function of **time**; they bear little relation to material or prime cost; material prices fluctuate, so rates swing; skill and machine use are ignored.

**ICAI example:** material ₹2,00,000, labour ₹1,00,000, overheads ₹90,000, so 45% of material or 30% of prime cost. A job with material ₹10,000 and labour ₹7,000 costs ₹21,500 on the material basis but ₹22,100 on the prime cost basis: same job, different cost. Labour hour example: ₹90,000 ÷ 1,50,000 hours = ₹0.60; a 795-hour job absorbs ₹477.

**A good method:** recovers about the actual total; is equitable (time spent, skilled vs unskilled, manual vs machine); convenient and stable from period to period.

## Machine hour rate

- **Direct MHR:** only expenses directly connected with the machine (power, depreciation, repairs, insurance) ÷ operating hours.
- **Comprehensive MHR:** also a share of departmental expenses (supervision, cleaning and lighting, consumables, general labour, rent). Sometimes operators' wages are added too.
- Split into **standing charges** (fixed: insurance, rent, supervisor's salary, time-based depreciation) and **machine expenses** (variable: power, consumables, repairs, activity-based depreciation).
- Steps: department overheads → share to the machine → add machine-specific costs → divide by productive hours.
- Advantages: best where machines dominate; under-absorption shows idle machine time; suits one operator on several machines or several operators on one. Disadvantages: extra records of machine time; extra work for separate rates.

### ICAI Illustration 6

Machine ₹1,00,00,000, life 10 years, scrap ₹9,00,000; lifetime repairs ₹18,00,000; 4,380 hours a year (365 a month); 15 units of power an hour at ₹5; a quarter of the area (rent ₹30,000 pm); 2 of 10 light points (lighting ₹8,000 pm); one-sixth of the foreman's time (₹19,200 pm); insurance 1% pa; oil ₹900 pm.

| Standing charges per month | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Depreciation (1,00,00,000 − 9,00,000) ÷ 120 months | 75,833 |
| Rent 30,000 × ¼ | 7,500 |
| Lighting 8,000 × 2/10 | 1,600 |
| Foreman 19,200 × 1/6 | 3,200 |
| Oil etc. | 900 |
| Insurance 1,00,000 ÷ 12 | 8,333 |
| **Total ₹97,366 ÷ 365 hours** | **₹266.76 per hour** |
| Repairs 18,00,000 ÷ 43,800 hours | 41.10 |
| Power 15 × ₹5 | 75.00 |
| **Machine hour rate** | **₹382.86** |

### ICAI Illustration 7 (comprehensive rate including operators)

Three equal machines, three operators each at ₹20 per hour; 48-hour week including 4 hours set-up; bonus 10% of productive time; thirteen four-week periods. Productive hours per period = 192 − 16 = 176.

- Standing charges: (rent 5,400 + heat and light 9,720 + foreman 12,960 + misc 18,000) = 46,080 ÷ (3 machines × 13) = 1,181.54; wages 48 × 4 × 20 × 3 = 11,520; bonus 176 × 20 × 3 × 10% = 1,056. Total **₹13,757.54**.
- Machine expenses: depreciation 52,000 × 10% ÷ 13 = 400; repairs 60 × 4 = 240; consumables 75 × 4 = 300; power 176 × 20 × 0.80 = 2,816 (none in set-up). Total **₹3,756**.
- Cost of running one machine for four weeks = **₹17,513.54**; MHR = 17,513.54 ÷ 176 = **₹99.51**.

## Types of overhead rate

| Rate | Formula / use |
|---|---|
| Normal (actual) | Actual overheads ÷ actual base |
| Pre-determined | Budgeted overheads ÷ budgeted base (from last year's rate, estimated expenses and activity, or normal volume) |
| Blanket | One rate for the whole factory: only one major product, or all products pass through all departments for the same time |
| Departmental | One rate per department: preferred where products or machine use vary |

## Under- and over-absorption

Absorbed = pre-determined rate × actual base. **Under-absorption:** absorbed < actual (debit balance). **Over-absorption:** absorbed > actual (credit balance). Causes: actual overheads differ from budget, actual output differs, or both change in different proportions (equal proportional changes leave no difference).

**Treatment:**
- **Small** amounts: to **Costing P&L Account**.
- **Large** amounts from wrong estimation (unavoidable reasons): **supplementary rate** = under/over-absorbed ÷ units produced, charged (or credited) to **finished goods stock, WIP (equivalent units) and cost of sales**.
- **Abnormal** reasons (abnormal idle capacity, inefficiency): **Costing P&L Account**.

### ICAI Illustration 8

Actual overheads ₹4,46,380; recovered at ₹1.25 × 2,93,104 hours = ₹3,66,380; **under-absorbed ₹80,000**. Output 7,800 units (7,000 sold) plus 200 equivalent units in WIP. Half due to higher prices of indirect material and labour; half due to factory inefficiency.

- ₹40,000 (price rise, a genuine cost): supplementary rate = 40,000 ÷ (7,800 + 200) = **₹5 per unit**: finished goods 800 × 5 = ₹4,000; WIP 200 × 5 = ₹1,000; cost of sales 7,000 × 5 = ₹35,000.
- ₹40,000 (inefficiency, abnormal): **Costing P&L Account**.

## Administration overheads

CIMA: costs of general management and secretarial, accounting and administrative services not directly related to production, marketing, research or development.

**Three accounting methods:**
1. **Apportion between production and selling:** they lose identity. Drawbacks: hard to find bases, clerical work, ignores other functions such as finance.
2. **Charge to Costing P&L:** policy costs, mostly fixed, hard to apportion. Drawbacks: product cost understated; against sound principle.
3. **Separate addition to cost of production or sales:** administration treated as its own function; bases: works cost, sales value or quantity, gross profit, quantity produced, conversion cost.

**Control:** functional classification and comparison with results (and with outside service costs); budgets; standards.

### ICAI Illustration 9: reverse calculation of rates

Factory overhead = F × direct wages; administration = A × factory cost. Job 101: material 54,000, wages 42,000, price 1,66,650 at 10% on cost → total cost 1,51,500. Job 102: 37,500, 30,000, price 1,28,250 at 20% on cost → total cost 1,06,875.

1. (96,000 + 42,000F)(1 + A) = 1,51,500 and (67,500 + 30,000F)(1 + A) = 1,06,875.
2. Multiply the first by 5 and the second by 7 and subtract: 7,500 + 7,500A = 9,375 → **A = 0.25**.
3. Then 1,20,000 + 52,500F = 1,51,500 → **F = 0.60**.
4. Job 101: factory OH 25,200; factory cost 1,21,200; admin 30,300; total 1,51,500; profit 15,150. Job 102: 18,000; 85,500; 21,375; 1,06,875; profit 21,375.
5. Job 103 (material 24,000, wages 20,000, profit 12½% on **selling price**): prime 44,000 + 12,000 = 56,000; + 14,000 = total cost 70,000; price = 70,000 ÷ 87.5% = **₹80,000**.

## Selling and distribution overheads

**Absorption bases:** sales value (usually the most logical), cost of goods sold, gross profit (ability to pay), number of orders or units. **Best:** split fixed and variable; apportion fixed by benefit (sales salaries by time spent per product; advertising by actual product spend; showroom by space; godown rent and own delivery vans by quantities delivered; otherwise by sales) to get fixed cost per unit; add variable cost per unit (packing, freight out, transit insurance, commission, rebates).

**Hard to control** because they depend on external factors (distance, competition, terms), customer behaviour, and are policy costs. Methods: comparison with past (as % of sales), budgetary control (flexible budget), standard costing (by salesman, territory, product).

### ICAI Illustration 10: product profitability with S&D overheads

| | A | B | C | D | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 30,00,000 | 50,00,000 | 25,00,000 | 45,00,000 | 1,50,00,000 |
| Variable (COGS + 4% commission + ₹2/parcel + ₹1/invoice) | 24,00,000 | 51,40,000 | 24,10,000 | 29,00,000 | 1,28,50,000 |
| **Contribution** | 6,00,000 | (1,40,000) | 90,000 | 16,00,000 | 21,50,000 |
| Fixed (rent by area 5:4:8:3; depreciation by parcels 4:6:3:7; salesmen by sales 6:10:5:9; admin by invoices 4:7:3:6) | 3,15,000 | 4,65,000 | 3,10,000 | 4,10,000 | 15,00,000 |
| **Profit / (loss)** | 2,85,000 | (6,05,000) | (2,20,000) | 11,90,000 | 6,50,000 |

B has a **negative contribution**, so it is the product to drop; C has a positive contribution despite its loss.

## Capacity

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Installed (rated, theoretical) | Maximum capacity by technical specification; not achievable normally |
| Practical (operating, net, available) | After repairs, breakdowns, set-ups, holidays, stock-taking; usually 80-90% of rated; a base for overhead rates |
| Normal | Average achievable under normal circumstances, after planned maintenance, shifts, holidays, normal idle time, batch changeover |
| Actual | Achieved in the period, as % of installed |
| Normal idle capacity | Installed − normal |
| Abnormal idle capacity | Normal − actual (when actual is lower) |

**Idle capacity cost:** unavoidable reasons (repairs, maintenance, changeover) → **supplementary rate** on capacity used; avoidable reasons (faulty planning, power failure) → **Costing P&L**; seasonal reasons → cost of production by **inflating overhead rates**.

## Special items (ICAI para 11)

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Interest and finance charges (including cash discount allowed) | Separate item in cost of sales; excludes imputed costs |
| Depreciation | Traced where feasible; otherwise cause and effect or benefit received |
| Packing | Primary → production cost; for transport → distribution; special at customer's request → that job; fancy (to attract) → selling (advertising) |
| Fringe benefits | Large: supplementary wage rate; otherwise production overhead |
| Removal and re-erection of machines | Production overhead (spread if large); faulty planning or abnormal → Costing P&L |
| Bad debts | Two views: excluded (financial loss), or selling and distribution overhead if normal; extraordinarily large → excluded |
| Training | Factory workers → production cost; office, sales, distribution → their overheads; abnormally high (high turnover) → Costing P&L |
| Canteen subsidy | Production overhead, by workers per department; share to office if office staff use it |
| Carriage and cartage | Direct material → material cost; stores → factory overhead; finished goods → distribution |
| Welfare activities | General overhead, apportioned by number of persons |
| Night-shift allowance | Production overhead; for a specific order → that order; abnormal → Costing P&L |
| R&D | Production methods → cost of production; administration research → admin; market research → selling; product development → that product (deferred, recovered per unit); fundamental research → admin; failed research → Costing P&L |

## Traps

- Under-absorbed overheads charged entirely to cost of sales. Spread a genuine shortfall over **finished stock, WIP and cost of sales**; send abnormal parts to Costing P&L.
- Profit on **selling price** vs on **cost** in reverse questions (Job 103: 12½% on price, so divide by 87.5%).
- Dropping a product with a loss but a positive contribution (product C).
- Computing MHR on total hours including set-up time when power is not used in set-up (Ill. 7 uses 176 productive hours).

## What to remember

- Best bases: machine hour rate (machine-intensive), labour hour rate (labour-intensive).
- Ill. 6 MHR ₹382.86; Ill. 7 ₹99.51.
- Under-absorption: small → Costing P&L; large genuine → supplementary rate on FG, WIP, cost of sales; abnormal → Costing P&L. Ill. 8: ₹5 per unit; ₹4,000 / ₹1,000 / ₹35,000; ₹40,000 to Costing P&L.
- Ill. 9: F 60% of wages, A 25% of factory cost; Job 103 price ₹80,000.
- Normal idle capacity = installed − normal; abnormal = normal − actual.

## Flashcards
Q: Why are % of material and % of prime cost methods unsuitable?
A: Overheads depend mainly on time, not on material or prime cost; material prices fluctuate; skill and machine use are ignored.

Q: Difference between direct and comprehensive machine hour rate?
A: Direct includes only expenses directly connected with the machine; comprehensive also includes a share of departmental expenses (and sometimes operators' wages).

Q: When is a blanket rate appropriate?
A: One major product, or all products pass through all departments for the same time.

Q: Formula for the supplementary rate?
A: Under- or over-absorbed overhead ÷ units produced (including equivalent WIP units).

Q: ICAI Ill. 8: under-absorbed ₹80,000, half from price rises, half from inefficiency. Treatment?
A: ₹40,000 by a supplementary rate of ₹5 per unit over FG (₹4,000), WIP (₹1,000) and cost of sales (₹35,000); ₹40,000 to Costing P&L.

Q: ICAI Ill. 6: machine hour rate?
A: ₹382.86 (standing charges ₹266.76 + repairs ₹41.10 + power ₹75).

Q: Normal idle capacity vs abnormal idle capacity?
A: Normal = installed − normal capacity; abnormal = normal − actual capacity.

Q: Idle capacity cost from seasonal factors: treatment?
A: Charged to cost of production by inflating overhead rates.

Q: Treatment of fancy packing?
A: Selling overhead (advertising).

Q: Treatment of failed research?
A: Excluded from cost, charged to Costing P&L.

Q: Three methods of accounting for administration overheads?
A: Apportion between production and selling; charge to Costing P&L; treat as a separate addition to cost of production or sales.
