# OV-2: Cost Concepts, Elements and Classification

Covers: cost objects, cost units, cost drivers, the elements of cost, and the classifications by nature, function, controllability, normality and for decision making (18 decision-making cost terms). Classification by behaviour has its own node (OV-3). **(ICAI SM ch. 1)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Classic MCQ territory: "the cost unit of the power sector is...", "which cost is ignored in a replacement decision?", "ticket counter at a railway station is an example of...". Also the vocabulary for every later chapter.

## Cost object, cost unit, cost driver

- **Cost object:** anything for which a separate measurement of cost is required: product (smartphone), service (Delhi-Mumbai flight), project (metro rail), activity (quality inspection), process (crude refining), department. Whether a cost is direct or indirect, fixed or variable, **depends on the cost object**.
- **Cost unit:** a unit of product, service or time (or a combination) in which cost is ascertained or expressed.

| Industry | Cost unit |
|---|---|
| Automobile | Number |
| Cement | Tonne / bag |
| Power, electricity | Kilowatt-hour (kWh) |
| Steel, coal mining | Tonne |
| Transport | Passenger-kilometre |
| Gas | Cubic feet |
| Brewing, oil | Barrel |
| Brick-making | 1,000 bricks |
| Hotel / catering | Room / meal |
| Hospital | Patient day |
| Professional services | Chargeable hour, job |
| Education | Course, enrolled student |

- **Cost driver:** a factor influencing the level of cost (CIMA). In ABC it links resource consumption to output, for example number of purchase orders for procurement cost, machine set-ups, inspection hours.

## Elements of cost (by nature)

| Element | Direct part | Indirect part |
|---|---|---|
| Material | In the product or economically identifiable (cloth in a dress) | Small quantities not worth tracing; ancillary materials |
| Employee (labour) | Workers converting material into product | Supervisors, maintenance staff |
| Other expenses | Hire of special machinery, cost of defective work | Rent, power |

**Overheads** = indirect material + indirect labour + indirect expenses, split into production (works), administration, selling and distribution overheads.

## Classification by function

Production / manufacturing; administration; selling; distribution; research and development. Flow: direct costs → prime cost → + factory overheads → works cost → + administration overheads → cost of goods sold → + selling and distribution → cost of sales. (Full layout: OV-5.)

## By controllability

- **Controllable:** can be influenced by the manager of that responsibility centre (direct costs, some overheads).
- **Uncontrollable:** cannot be influenced by that person. Tool-room cost is controllable by the tool-room foreman, but its share apportioned to the machine shop is not controllable by the machine-shop foreman. **No cost is uncontrollable in itself; it is always relative to a person.**

## By normality

- **Normal cost:** normally incurred at a given output under normal conditions. Part of product cost.
- **Abnormal cost:** not normally incurred; charged to the **Costing Profit and Loss Account**.

## For managerial decision making

| Term | Meaning / example |
|---|---|
| Pre-determined | Computed in advance from the factors affecting cost |
| Standard | Pre-determined cost at an expected standard of efficient operation; basis for variance analysis |
| Marginal | Change in total cost when output changes by one unit |
| Estimated | Expected cost computed in advance (Kohler); prospective |
| Differential | Change in total cost (fixed and variable) from a change in activity, technology or method; incremental or decremental |
| Imputed | Notional, no cash outlay (interest on own capital); like opportunity cost |
| Capitalised | First recorded as assets, later expensed (machine installation) |
| Product | Inventoriable: variable manufacturing cost under marginal costing; total manufacturing cost under absorption costing |
| Opportunity | Benefit foregone (interest lost on deposits withdrawn for expansion) |
| Out-of-pocket | Involves cash outflow; short-run; used in recession pricing, make or buy; avoidable if the proposal is dropped |
| Shut-down | Continue during a temporary shutdown (rent, rates, depreciation) |
| Sunk | Past, irrelevant to decisions (WDV of the old machine in a replacement decision) |
| Absolute | Cost in total, the base for further analysis |
| Discretionary | No clear input-output link; periodic decisions (advertising, PR, training) |
| Period | Charged to the period, not to products (administration, selling and distribution) |
| Engineered | Clear, observable input-output link (direct material, direct labour) |
| Explicit | Immediate cash payment (salaries, postage, interest on loan) |
| Implicit | No cash payment, not in the books; economic cost |

## Traps

- Treating sunk cost as relevant. It never affects a current decision.
- Mixing discretionary (no input-output link) with engineered (clear link).
- Charging abnormal cost to product cost. It goes to the Costing P&L Account.
- Quoting "passenger-kilometre" for goods transport. Goods transport uses tonne-kilometre (see Service Costing, MC-6).

## What to remember

- Cost unit table: power kWh, transport passenger-km, hospital patient day, brick 1,000 bricks.
- Overheads = indirect material + indirect labour + indirect expenses.
- Controllability is always relative to a person.
- Sunk ignored; opportunity and imputed counted in decisions though not in the books.

## Flashcards
Q: Cost unit for the power sector?
A: Kilowatt-hour (kWh).

Q: Cost unit for a hospital?
A: Patient day.

Q: What is a cost driver?
A: A factor influencing the level of cost, for example number of purchase orders for procurement cost.

Q: Where is abnormal cost charged?
A: To the Costing Profit and Loss Account, not to product cost.

Q: In a machine replacement decision, how is the WDV of the old machine treated?
A: As a sunk cost: ignored.

Q: Difference between discretionary and engineered cost?
A: Discretionary has no clear input-output relationship (advertising); engineered has a clear, observable one (direct material).

Q: What are shut-down costs?
A: Fixed costs that continue during a temporary closure, such as rent, rates and depreciation.

Q: What is product cost under marginal costing and under absorption costing?
A: Variable manufacturing cost under marginal costing; total manufacturing cost (variable and fixed) under absorption costing.

Q: Is tool-room cost apportioned to the machine shop controllable by the machine-shop foreman?
A: No. It is controllable only by the tool-room foreman.
