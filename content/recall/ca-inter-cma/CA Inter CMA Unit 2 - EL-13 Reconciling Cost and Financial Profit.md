# EL-13: Reconciling Cost and Financial Profit

Covers: why the two profits differ (financial-only items, cost-only notional items, different treatment, different stock valuation), the three-step procedure, the memorandum reconciliation account, and ICAI Illustrations 6 and 7. **(ICAI SM ch. 7, para 4)**

## Where this fits in the exam

A reconciliation statement is one of the most predictable CMA questions (5-10 marks). The skill is deciding, for each item, whether it **raises or lowers** the profit you start from.

## Why profits differ

1. **Financial accounts only.**
   - Purely financial expenses: interest on loans or mortgages; expenses and discounts on issuing shares and debentures; capital losses (uninsured fire loss); losses on sale of fixed assets and investments; income tax, donations, subscriptions; share transfer office expenses.
   - Purely financial income: interest on deposits, loans and investments; dividends; profit on sale of fixed assets and investments; transfer fees; rent receivable.
2. **Cost accounts only (notional):** rent charged for owned premises; notional interest on capital; notional proprietor's salary; notional depreciation on fully depreciated assets.
3. **Different treatment:** LIFO allowed in cost books but not by accounting standards; different depreciation methods; **under- or over-absorption** of overheads (cost books charge absorbed amounts, financial books actual amounts).
4. **Different stock valuation:** financial accounts at lower of cost and market; cost accounts at cost only.

## Procedure

1. Find the profit as per financial accounts.
2. Find the profit as per cost accounts.
3. Reconcile through a **Memorandum Reconciliation Account** or statement: start from one profit and add or subtract each difference (like a bank reconciliation).

No reconciliation is needed when accounts are **integrated**.

## The sign rules (starting from **costing** profit)

| Item | Effect on financial profit vs cost profit | Starting from cost profit |
|---|---|---|
| Overheads **over**-absorbed in cost books | Cost books charged more than actual | **Add** |
| Overheads **under**-absorbed | Cost books charged less | **Subtract** |
| Financial-only **expenses** (interest, goodwill written off, loss on sale) | Only in financial books | **Subtract** |
| Financial-only **income** (dividends, interest received, profit on sale) | Only in financial books | **Add** |
| Notional costs (rent on own premises, interest on capital) | Only in cost books | **Add** |
| Closing stock valued **higher** in financial books | Financial profit higher | **Add** |
| Opening stock valued **higher** in financial books | Financial profit lower | **Subtract** |

Starting from financial profit, reverse every sign.

## ICAI Illustration 6: ABC Manufacturing

Financial P&L: sales (20,000 units) 25,00,000 + closing FG 1,50,000 + closing WIP 70,000 = 27,20,000; less materials 10,00,000, wages 5,00,000, factory OH 4,50,000, admin OH (production) 2,60,000, S&D 1,80,000, goodwill written off 2,00,000, interest on loan 20,000. **Financial profit = ₹1,10,000.**

Cost records: factory OH 100% of wages; admin 10% of factory cost; S&D ₹10 per unit sold.

| Cost sheet | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Prime cost (10,00,000 + 5,00,000) | 15,00,000 |
| Factory OH 100% of wages | 5,00,000 |
| Less closing WIP | (70,000) |
| Factory cost (21,230 units) | 19,30,000 |
| Admin 10% | 1,93,000 |
| | 21,23,000 |
| Less closing FG (21,23,000 × 1,230 ÷ 21,230) | (1,23,000) |
| Cost of goods sold | 20,00,000 |
| S&D 20,000 × ₹10 | 2,00,000 |
| Cost of sales | 22,00,000 |
| **Costing profit** (25,00,000 − 22,00,000) | **3,00,000** |

**Reconciliation**

| | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Profit as per cost accounts | 3,00,000 |
| Add: factory OH over-absorbed (5,00,000 − 4,50,000) | 50,000 |
| Add: S&D over-absorbed (2,00,000 − 1,80,000) | 20,000 |
| Add: closing FG higher in financial books (1,50,000 − 1,23,000) | 27,000 |
| Less: admin under-absorbed (2,60,000 − 1,93,000) | (67,000) |
| Less: goodwill written off | (2,00,000) |
| Less: interest on loan | (20,000) |
| **Profit as per financial accounts** | **1,10,000** |

## ICAI Illustration 7: from the cost ledger

Stores: opening 15,000 + purchases 80,000 + returned from WIP 40,000; issued to WIP 80,000, to repairs 10,000, sold at cost 5,000, shortage 3,000 (treated as normal, to overhead); closing **₹37,000**. Wages paid 35,000: 30,000 direct, 5,000 indirect. Overhead control: repairs material 10,000 + shortage 3,000 + expenses 1,25,000 + indirect wages 5,000 = **₹1,43,000 incurred**, absorbed ₹1,20,000. WIP: opening 30,000 + material 80,000 + wages 30,000 + overhead 1,20,000 − returned to stores 40,000 − closing 20,000 = **finished output ₹2,00,000**, sold at 10% on cost = sales ₹2,20,000.

- **Costing profit = ₹20,000.**
- Financial P&L: materials (15,000 + 80,000 − 5,000 sold) 90,000 + opening WIP 30,000 + wages 35,000 + overheads 1,25,000 = 2,80,000; credits: sales 2,20,000 + closing WIP 20,000 + closing raw material 37,000 = 2,77,000. **Financial loss = ₹3,000.**
- Reconciliation: financial loss (3,000) + **₹23,000** overhead difference = costing profit 20,000.

Note: overhead incurred ₹1,43,000 vs absorbed ₹1,20,000 is an **under-absorption** of ₹23,000 (the cost books charged less, so costing profit is higher); ICAI's printed solution labels it "over absorbed", but the figure and the direction of the adjustment are what matter: starting from the financial result, it is added.

## Traps

- Getting the sign backwards. Ask: "did this make the financial profit higher or lower than the cost profit?"
- Forgetting stock valuation differences (ICAI Ill. 6: ₹27,000 on closing FG).
- Treating notional rent or interest as a financial expense. They exist **only in cost books**, so starting from cost profit you add them back.
- Including interest on loans in cost. It appears only in financial accounts.

## What to remember

- Four causes: financial-only items, notional (cost-only) items, different treatment (absorption, LIFO, depreciation), stock valuation.
- From cost profit: add over-absorption, financial income, notional costs, higher financial closing stock; subtract under-absorption, financial expenses.
- Ill. 6: cost 3,00,000 → financial 1,10,000. Ill. 7: financial (3,000) → cost 20,000 (overhead difference ₹23,000).

## Flashcards
Q: Name two items found only in financial accounts.
A: Any two of: interest on loans, goodwill written off, loss on sale of assets, income tax, donations, dividends received, interest received, profit on sale of investments.

Q: Name two notional items found only in cost accounts.
A: Rent on owned premises, notional interest on capital, notional proprietor's salary, notional depreciation on fully depreciated assets.

Q: Starting from costing profit, how is over-absorbed overhead treated?
A: Added.

Q: Starting from costing profit, how is interest on loan treated?
A: Subtracted.

Q: How do stock valuation bases differ between the two sets of accounts?
A: Financial: lower of cost and market; cost: at cost.

Q: ICAI Ill. 6: costing profit and financial profit?
A: ₹3,00,000 and ₹1,10,000.

Q: When is a reconciliation not needed?
A: When cost and financial accounts are integrated.

Q: ICAI Ill. 6: closing FG valued at ₹1,50,000 in financial books and ₹1,23,000 in cost books. Effect starting from cost profit?
A: Add ₹27,000.
