# OV-1: Objectives, Scope and Related Disciplines

Covers: the ICAI definitions (cost, costing, cost accounting, cost accountancy, management accounting, cost management), the five objectives, cost control vs cost reduction, the seven-part scope, how cost accounting relates to financial accounting, management accounting and financial management, functions, users, essentials of a good system, installation, responsibility centres and limitations. **(ICAI SM ch. 1)** Background: [[Management Accounting Unit 1 - FM-1 Meaning Scope and Importance]] covers the same ground at BBA level; use the ICAI wording below in the exam.

## Where this fits in the exam

Pure theory, 10-15% of the paper together with the rest of Unit 1. It turns up as MCQs (cost control vs cost reduction, users, responsibility centres) and as 4-5 mark descriptive parts in Part II ("DESCRIBE the factors to be considered before installing a costing system").

## Definitions (ICAI wording)

- **Cost:** as a noun, the amount of expenditure (actual or notional) incurred on or attributable to a specified article, product or activity. As a verb, to ascertain the cost of a specified thing or activity.
- **Costing:** the technique and process of ascertaining costs.
- **Cost accounting:** the process of accounting for cost, beginning with the recording of income and expenditure and ending with periodical statements and reports for ascertaining and controlling costs.
- **Cost accountancy:** applying costing and cost accounting principles, methods and techniques to the science, art and practice of cost control and the ascertainment of profitability, including presenting the information for decision making.
- **Management accounting (CIMA):** applying accounting and financial management principles to create, protect, preserve and increase value for stakeholders.
- **Cost management:** applying management accounting concepts to plan, monitor and control costs.

## Five objectives

1. **Ascertainment of cost** for each cost object (unit, job, process, department, service).
2. **Determination of selling price and profitability** (a basis for price fixing and rate negotiation).
3. **Cost control:** set a pre-determined standard, measure actual performance on the same basis, compare, analyse the variance and act.
4. **Cost reduction:** "real and permanent reduction in the unit cost ... without impairing suitability for the use intended or diminution in quality". Three assumptions: a saving in unit cost; the saving is permanent; utility and quality are unaffected or improved. Done by removing non-value-added activities (value chain analysis) and continuous research.
5. **Assisting management in decision making.**

## Cost control vs cost reduction (a favourite)

| Cost control | Cost reduction |
|---|---|
| Keeps costs in line with established standards | Challenges the standards and keeps improving them |
| Lowest cost under existing conditions | Treats no condition as permanent |
| Emphasis on past and present | Emphasis on present and future |
| Preventive function | Corrective function; works even when control is efficient |
| Ends when targets are met | No visible end; continuous |

## Scope (seven functions)

Costing; cost accounting; cost analysis (finding why actual differs from budget, fixing responsibility); cost comparisons (alternative technologies, products, periods); cost control; cost reports (the ultimate function); statutory compliances (cost records under the Act and rules).

## Relationship with other disciplines

**Cost vs management accounting**

| Basis | Cost accounting | Management accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Quantitative only | Qualitative and quantitative |
| Objective | Cost of products and services | Information for planning and co-ordination |
| Area | Cost ascertainment | Wider: includes financial accounting, budgeting, tax, planning |
| Data | Past and present | Projections for the future |
| Development | Industrial revolution | Needs of modern business |
| Rules | Follows set principles | No specific rules |

**Financial vs cost accounting**

| Basis | Financial accounting | Cost accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Financial performance of the entity | Cost for control and decisions |
| Data | Historical | Historical and pre-determined |
| Users | Shareholders, creditors, analysts, government | Mainly internal management (sometimes regulators) |
| Analysis | Profit for the whole entity or segments | Cost per cost object (product, job, process) |
| Period | Usually a year | As and when required |
| Format | Set format | No set format |

**Financial management:** cost and management accounting is an application of financial management for decision making. Picture cost accounting at the centre, inside management accounting, which overlaps with financial accounting, all within financial management.

## Role, functions and users

- **Functions:** collect cost by element; assign costs to cost objects; set budgets and standards and report deviations; provide information through the MIS; measure responsibility centre performance (time taken, wastage, idleness).
- **Internal users:** policy makers, managers, operational staff, employees. **External users:** regulators (tariffs, subsidies), auditors (including cost audit), shareholders, creditors and lenders.

## Essentials of a good costing system

Informative and simple; accurate and authentic; uniform and consistent; integrated and inclusive; flexible and adaptive; management trust in the system.

## Installing a costing system: factors to study first

Objective (pricing or control?); nature of the business (an oil refinery needs process accounts); organisational hierarchy (each level's information needs); knowing the product (by-products, perishables suit marginal costing); knowing the production process; information synchronisation with other departments (stock statements to banks, tax returns); method of maintaining records (interlocking or integral, reconciliation through control accounts); statutory compliance and cost accounting standards; information attributes (complete, accurate, timely, relevant).

## Responsibility centres

| Centre | Accountable for | Example / measure |
|---|---|---|
| Cost centre: standard | Costs, where the input-output ratio is clear | Compared with standard cost |
| Cost centre: discretionary | Costs, where output cannot be measured in money | R&D, advertising; compared with budget |
| Revenue centre | Revenue | Sales department |
| Profit centre | Revenue and costs | Decentralised branches; profit |
| Investment centre | Profit plus capital investment decisions | ROI; Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna PSUs |

## Limitations

Expensive (extra work to analyse and absorb overheads); results must be reconciled with financial accounts; duplication (two sets of accounts).

## Traps

- Calling cost control "corrective". ICAI: control is **preventive**, reduction is **corrective**.
- Saying cost accounting uses only historical data. It uses historical **and** pre-determined data.
- Putting R&D in a standard cost centre. It is the textbook **discretionary** cost centre.

## What to remember

- Five objectives: ascertain, price, control, reduce, decide.
- Cost reduction: unit saving, permanent, quality intact.
- Control = preventive, ends at target; reduction = corrective, never ends.
- Investment centre measured by ROI.

## Flashcards
Q: ICAI definition of costing?
A: The technique and process of ascertaining costs.

Q: Three assumptions in the definition of cost reduction?
A: A saving in unit cost; the saving is permanent; utility and quality are unaffected, if not improved.

Q: Is cost control preventive or corrective?
A: Preventive. Cost reduction is corrective.

Q: Four steps of cost control?
A: Set a pre-determined standard; measure actual performance on the same basis; compare; analyse the variance and act.

Q: Which cost centre has no measurable input-output ratio?
A: A discretionary cost centre (for example R&D, advertising).

Q: How is an investment centre's performance measured?
A: Return on investment, besides profit.

Q: Three limitations of cost accounting?
A: Expensive, needs reconciliation with financial accounts, duplicates work.

Q: Name four factors to study before installing a costing system.
A: Any four of: objective, nature of business, organisational hierarchy, the product, the production process, information synchronisation, method of maintaining records, statutory compliance, information attributes.
