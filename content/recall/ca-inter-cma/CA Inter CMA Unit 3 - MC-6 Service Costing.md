# MC-6: Service Costing (Operating Costing)

Covers: internal and external uses; service vs product costing; cost units and KPIs by industry; composite units (absolute and commercial tonne-km); equivalent service units; the service cost statement (standing, running, maintenance; depreciation and interest); and costing for transport, hotels, hospitals, IT and ITES, toll roads (BOT), educational institutions, insurance, financial institutions and power houses; with ICAI Illustrations 1, 2, 6 and 10. **(ICAI SM ch. 12)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Service costing is a frequent 10-14 mark question (transport cost per passenger-km, hotel room rent with equivalent units and a profit on takings, toll rate per vehicle). Cost units and KPIs are MCQ favourites.

## Basics

Also called **operating costing**. **Internal** use: in-house support services (staff canteen, boiler house, captive power, vehicle fleet, IT, R&D, lab). **External** use: services sold to customers as a profit centre (transport, hotels, banks, insurance, IT).

**Service vs product costing:** services are intangible and cannot be stored (no inventory); **composite cost units**; employee cost usually exceeds material cost; indirect (support) costs are a large share and hard to trace.

## Cost units (ICAI)

| Service | Cost unit |
|---|---|
| Passenger transport | Passenger-km |
| Goods transport | Tonne-km or quintal-km |
| Electricity | kWh |
| Hospital | Patient per day, room or bed per day, per operation (out-patient: per patient; scanning: per case; laundry: per 100 items) |
| Canteen | Per item, per meal |
| Cinema | Per ticket |
| Hotel | Guest-days or (occupied) room-days |
| Bank, financial institution | Per transaction or service (letter of credit, application, project) |
| Education | Per course, student, batch, lecture |
| IT and ITES | Per project, per module (effort per person-day, week or month) |
| Insurance | Per policy, per claim, per TPA |

## KPIs (ICAI examples)

Transport: number of shipments, truck turnaround, lead (order cycle) time, on-time-in-full (OTIF). Hotels: cost per occupied room (CPOR), occupancy rate, RevPAR. Hospitals: bed occupancy, staff-to-patient ratio, average treatment charge. IT and ITES: gross burn rate, customer acquisition cost, customer lifetime value, monthly recurring revenue, churn, cost per feature. Telecom: ARPU, subscriber acquisition cost, network operating cost, gross revenue retention. Education: instructional cost, administrative cost per student, tuition cost, student-to-faculty ratio. Insurance: average cost per claim, components of claim costs, cost per quote, administrative cost per policy, average policy size.

## Composite units

- **Absolute (weighted average) tonne-km** = Σ (weight × distance) for each leg.
- **Commercial (simple average) tonne-km** = total distance × average load.

### ICAI Illustration 1

Lorry: A → B (80 km) with 20 MT, unloads 8; B → C (120 km) with 12 MT; C → A (160 km) with 16 MT.
- Absolute = 20 × 80 + 12 × 120 + 16 × 160 = 1,600 + 1,440 + 2,560 = **5,600 MT-km**.
- Commercial = (20 + 12 + 16) ÷ 3 × (80 + 120 + 160) = 16 × 360 = **5,760 MT-km**.

## Equivalent service units

Different grades sharing common resources are converted to one grade by weights. ICAI hotel example: Standard 100 rooms, Deluxe 50 at 2.5 × Standard, Luxurious 30 at 2 × Deluxe (= 5 × Standard); 360 days, full occupancy: Standard 36,000 + Deluxe 18,000 × 2.5 = 45,000 + Luxurious 10,800 × 5 = 54,000 → **1,35,000 standard room-days** (or 27,000 luxurious room-days).

## Service cost statement

By function or by variability:
1. **Standing charges** (fixed): insurance, licence, monthly salaries, garage rent, time-based depreciation, taxes, administration.
2. **Running charges** (variable): fuel, lubricants, operation-based wages, activity-based depreciation.
3. **Maintenance** (semi-variable): repairs, tyres, spares.

**Depreciation:** time-based = fixed; usage-based = variable. **Interest:** separate item of cost of sales; generally treated as fixed.

**Methods in services:** job costing for customised software or healthcare; process costing for utilities (power, water); joint-product costing for bundled services (telecom, events, education).

## Transport: ICAI Illustration 2

5 buses, 40 km apart, 40 seats, 75% occupancy, one round trip a day, 30 days. Costs ₹1,44,000 (crew 24,000; supervisor 10,000; diesel 40,000; repairs 8,000; tax and insurance 16,000; depreciation 26,000; interest 20,000).

1. Passenger-km = 5 × 40 × 40 × 75% × 30 × 2 = **3,60,000**.
2. Standing 96,000 (₹0.267); running 40,000 (₹0.111); maintenance 8,000 (₹0.022).
3. **Cost per passenger-km = ₹0.40**.

## Hotels: ICAI Illustration 6

Holiday home: rent ₹10,000 a month **plus 5% of total takings**. Single 100 rooms (100% occupied), double 50 (80%), triple 30 (60%); double = 2.5 × single; triple = 2 × double. Other expenses ₹25,21,000. Profit 20% of takings; 360 days.

1. Equivalent single room-days: 36,000 + 14,400 × 2.5 = 36,000 + 6,480 × 5 = 32,400 → **1,04,400**.
2. Cost = 25,21,000 + 1,20,000 = 26,41,000 + 5% of takings.
3. Takings = 26,41,000 + 25% of takings (5% rent + 20% profit) → takings = 26,41,000 ÷ 0.75 = **₹35,21,333**.
4. Single room rent = 35,21,333 ÷ 1,04,400 = **₹33.73**; double **₹84.33**; triple **₹168.65**.

## Hospitals

Departments: out-patient, in-patient, medical services (X-ray, scanning), general services (catering, laundry, power house), miscellaneous (transport, dispensary). Fixed: staff salaries, depreciation of building and equipment. Variable: laundry, patients' food, power.

## IT and ITES

Labour intensive; jobs are **projects** (each unique). Planning breaks a project into activities; **effort estimation** gives skill levels and durations. **Direct manpower:** engineers, consultants, analysts, project leaders and managers (traceable). **Support manpower:** QA, testing, version control, staffing managers: direct if traceable, otherwise overhead apportioned. Effort cost per person-day, week or month, and it includes salary **plus a share of common overhead** (office space, support staff, networking, central facilities, social security and benefits). Hardware, software, travel and training: direct if project-specific, otherwise service overhead (e.g. Java training useful across projects).

## Toll roads and BOT

Capital cost: preliminary and pre-operative expenses, land acquisition, materials, labour, construction overheads, contingency, **interest during construction**. Operating and maintenance: toll collection, administration, routine and periodic maintenance, interest on term loans. **BOT:** the private sector designs, finances, builds and operates; ownership passes to the Government after the concession period. Toll rate relates to user benefits (fuel savings, time, riding quality); **user fee = total distance × toll rate per km**, rounded to the nearest multiple of five.

### ICAI Illustration 10

60 km highway; 12 crore vehicles over 10 years = **10 lakh vehicles a month**. April costs: collection staff 3 shifts × 4 × 30 × 550 = 1,98,000; supervisors 2 × 1 × 30 × 750 = 45,000; security 3 × 6 × 30 × 450 = 2,43,000; booth manager 2 × 1 × 30 × 900 = 54,000; electricity 8,00,000; telephone 1,40,000; maintenance 30,00,000; operating total ₹44,80,000; depreciation and amortisation ₹1,50,00,000. **Total ₹1,94,80,000.**
- Cost per km per month = 1,94,80,000 ÷ 60 = **₹3,24,666.67**.
- Toll per vehicle = (1,94,80,000 × 125%) ÷ 10,00,000 = **₹24.35**.

## Educational institutions

Income: one-time fees (admission, development, annual), recurring fees (tuition, lab, library, sports), other (transport, hostel, mess on no-profit basis); aided institutions cannot charge some fees and face fee limits. Costs: teaching and non-teaching salaries, lab, computers and internet, building and equipment upkeep, administration, finance. **Cost centres:** primary (departments), service (lab, library, sports), students' self-supporting services (transport, hostel, mess), administration (research, examination). Research costs are pooled, set off against research revenue, and any balance spread over course cost centres as "research costs".

## Insurance and financial institutions

**Insurance** income: premiums, reinsurance commission, fund administration fees and returns. Direct costs: agents' commission, claim settlement, valuation, reinsurance premium, legal. Indirect: actuarial fees, market and product development, administration, asset management. Cost objects: product, policy, department, region, agent. **ABC** is used for product profitability and customer (policy) profitability, with activities split into pre- and post-product development.

**Financial institutions:** manpower is the largest cost after interest. Cost measurement aims to show profitability by product and customer, set prices from activity-level unit costs, and link productivity to strategy. ABC identifies product and customer profitability and builds product costs from activity costs; focus on profitable products and high-margin customers.

## Power houses

Cost unit **kWh** (a "unit"); for steam, per unit of steam. Standing: rent, rates, taxes, insurance, depreciation, time-paid salaries, administration. Running: fuel, water, production-based wages. Maintenance (semi-variable): meters, furnaces, service materials, tools.

## Traps

- Counting a round trip as one trip: passenger-km uses **2 single trips** per round trip.
- Absolute vs commercial tonne-km: absolute sums each leg; commercial uses average load × total distance.
- Profit "on takings" and rent "% of takings" are both percentages of revenue: gross up by dividing by (1 − 25%).
- Toll: divide total estimated vehicles over the tenure into **per month** before dividing cost.

## What to remember

- Composite units: absolute 5,600 vs commercial 5,760 MT-km (Ill. 1).
- Bus: 3,60,000 passenger-km, ₹0.40 each (Ill. 2).
- Hotel: 1,04,400 equivalent room-days; single ₹33.73, double ₹84.33, triple ₹168.65 (Ill. 6).
- Toll: ₹3,24,666.67 per km per month; ₹24.35 per vehicle (Ill. 10).

## Flashcards
Q: Another name for service costing?
A: Operating costing.

Q: Four ways service costing differs from product costing?
A: Intangible (no inventory); composite cost units; employee cost exceeds material; indirect costs significant and hard to trace.

Q: Formula for absolute tonne-km?
A: Σ (weight carried × distance) for each leg.

Q: Formula for commercial tonne-km?
A: Total distance × average load.

Q: ICAI Ill. 1: absolute and commercial MT-km?
A: 5,600 and 5,760.

Q: Cost unit for a hotel?
A: Guest-days or occupied room-days.

Q: What does RevPAR mean?
A: Revenue per available room (per available room-day).

Q: Time-based depreciation in a transport cost sheet: fixed or variable?
A: Fixed (standing charge); usage-based depreciation is variable.

Q: What is BOT?
A: Build-Operate-Transfer: the private sector designs, finances, builds and operates a facility, then transfers ownership to the Government after the concession period.

Q: ICAI Ill. 10: toll per vehicle?
A: ₹24.35.

Q: In IT costing, what does effort cost include besides salary?
A: A share of common overheads: office space, support staff, networking, central facilities, social security and benefits.
