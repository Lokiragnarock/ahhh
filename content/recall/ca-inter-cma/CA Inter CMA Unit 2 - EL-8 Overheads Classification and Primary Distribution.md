# EL-8: Overheads: Classification and Primary Distribution

Covers: classification of overheads (function, nature or behaviour, element, control); why fixed and variable are separated (with ICAI's flexible budget example); the four stages of accounting for manufacturing overheads; standing orders; departmentalisation; allocation vs apportionment; ICAI's table of apportionment bases and the other principles (survey, ability to pay, efficiency). **(ICAI SM ch. 4, paras 1-4.3)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Overheads are the single heaviest chapter in Paper 4. Primary distribution is the first step of most overhead questions (a primary distribution summary feeding secondary distribution and machine hour rates). MCQs test apportionment bases ("rent: floor area", "PF contribution: direct wages").

## Classification of overheads

| Basis | Classes and ICAI examples |
|---|---|
| **Function** | Factory (stock-keeping, plant repairs, factory depreciation, indirect labour, **primary packing**, plant insurance; includes production administration). Office and administration (office salaries, office building repairs and depreciation, postage, operating lease rent, audit). Selling (commission, advertising, sales office). Distribution (delivery vans, transit insurance, warehouse and cold storage, **secondary packing**) |
| **Nature (behaviour)** | Fixed (permanent salaries, depreciation, interest on capital, insurance). Variable (indirect materials, power and fuel, lubricants, tools and spares). Semi-variable (electricity, water, telephone and internet) |
| **Element** | Indirect materials (lubricants, cotton waste, service department stores). Indirect employee cost (foremen, supervisors, administration staff). Indirect expenses (rates and taxes, insurance, depreciation, advertising) |
| **Control** | Controllable (materials, wages, power). Uncontrollable (rates and taxes, depreciation, interest on borrowings) |

## Why separate fixed and variable

1. **Control:** fixed costs are policy costs; variable costs are controlled by allowances related to output.
2. **Budgets:** flexible budgets at different activity levels.
3. **Decisions:** pricing in recession or for export, make or buy, shut down or continue; the basis of marginal costing and CVP.

**ICAI example (October to November 2025):** 1,000 units: fixed ₹5,00,000; variable ₹4,00,000; semi-variable ₹6,00,000 (40% fixed). Budget for 1,200 units: fixed 5,00,000 + variable 4,80,000 + semi-variable fixed 2,40,000 + semi-variable variable (3,60,000 × 1.2) 4,32,000 = **₹16,52,000**, not ₹18,00,000 (which wrongly scales everything by 1.2).

## Four stages for manufacturing overheads

Actual overheads are known only after the books close, so overheads are charged through **pre-determined rates** and the difference adjusted later.

1. **Estimation and collection:** past figures adjusted for known changes; sources: invoices, stores requisitions, wage analysis book, journal entries; collected through **standing orders**.
2. **Assignment**, on the principles of **cause and effect** and **benefit received**:
   - **Allocation:** direct assignment of a whole cost to a cost centre (power from a department's own meter).
   - **Apportionment:** sharing a common cost over centres (works manager's salary).
   - **Re-apportionment (secondary distribution):** service department costs moved to production departments (EL-9).
3. **Absorption:** recovering each production department's overheads from its output. **Variable overheads on actual production; fixed overheads on normal capacity.**
4. **Under- or over-absorption:** actual vs absorbed, adjusted at year end (EL-10).

Purpose: charge each product an equitable share, and charge it as soon as the job is done.

## Standing orders

A "standing order" is a sanction for indirect expenses under a head, with a permanent number allotted to each expense head and kept year to year (production orders authorise direct costs; standing orders authorise indirect ones).

## Departmentalisation

Overheads collected by department or cost centre (personal or machine cost centres, often coded). **Advantages:** better estimates; better control (who overspent); departmental cost for each job (jobs pass through departments differently); a suitable costing method per department (batch costing for parts, output costing for assembly).

## Apportionment bases (ICAI table)

| Overhead | Basis |
|---|---|
| Rent and building expenses, lighting and heating, fire precaution, air-conditioning | Floor area or volume |
| Perquisites, labour welfare, time-keeping, personnel office, supervision | Number of workers |
| Workers' compensation, holiday pay, ESI and PF contribution | Direct wages |
| General overhead | Direct labour hours, direct wages or machine hours |
| Depreciation, repairs and maintenance of plant; insurance of stock | Capital values |
| Power or steam consumption, internal transport, managerial salaries | Technical estimates |
| Lighting | Light points, area or metered units |
| Electric power (machines) | Horse power, machine hours, machine value or units consumed |
| Material handling, stores overhead | Weight, volume, value or units of materials |

**Other principles:** analysis or survey of existing conditions (light points); **ability to pay** (sales organisation cost by sales of each product; simple but can be inequitable, as easy-selling lines bear the most); efficiency or incentive (pre-set production or sales levels; exceeding them lowers unit overhead).

## Allocation vs apportionment

| Allocation | Apportionment |
|---|---|
| Whole items of cost identifiable with one centre | Proportions of a cost shared by centres |
| Direct process | Indirect process (needs a basis) |

Whether a cost is allocated or apportioned depends on its **relationship to the cost centre**, not its nature. Allocation is the wider term.

## Traps

- Scaling semi-variable cost fully with output. Only its variable part moves.
- Absorbing fixed overheads on actual production. ICAI: fixed on **normal capacity**, variable on actual production.
- Apportioning PF contribution by number of workers. ICAI: by **direct wages**.
- Rent by number of workers. Rent goes by **floor area**.

## What to remember

- Four stages: estimate and collect → allocate, apportion, re-apportion → absorb → adjust under/over absorption.
- Allocation = whole cost to one centre; apportionment = shared cost on a basis.
- Bases: rent area; welfare and supervision workers; PF and ESI direct wages; depreciation capital value; power HP × hours.
- ICAI flexible budget example: ₹16,52,000 at 1,200 units, not ₹18,00,000.

## Flashcards
Q: Two principles for assigning manufacturing overheads?
A: Cause and effect, and benefit received.

Q: Difference between allocation and apportionment?
A: Allocation charges a whole cost directly to one cost centre; apportionment shares a common cost over centres on a suitable basis.

Q: Basis for absorbing fixed manufacturing overheads per ICAI?
A: Normal capacity (variable overheads on actual production).

Q: Basis for apportioning rent?
A: Floor area (or volume).

Q: Basis for apportioning employer's PF and ESI contribution?
A: Direct wages.

Q: Basis for depreciation and repairs of plant?
A: Capital values.

Q: What is a standing order?
A: A sanction for indirect expenses under a head, with a permanent number allotted to each expense head.

Q: What is the "ability to pay" basis and its drawback?
A: Apportioning by the revenue of each product or department; it may be inequitable because easy-selling lines bear the most.

Q: ICAI example: fixed 5L, variable 4L, semi-variable 6L (40% fixed) at 1,000 units. Budget at 1,200 units?
A: ₹16,52,000.
