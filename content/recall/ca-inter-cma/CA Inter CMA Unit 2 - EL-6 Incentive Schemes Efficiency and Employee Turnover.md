# EL-6: Incentive Schemes, Efficiency Rating and Employee Turnover

Covers: time rate and piece rate; the Halsey and Rowan premium bonus plans with their formulas, merits and the point where they pay the same; efficiency rating and productivity; employee turnover by the replacement, separation and flux methods, annualising, causes, effects and cost, with ICAI Illustrations 6-11, 14 and 15. **(ICAI SM ch. 3, paras 8, 10, 11)**

## Where this fits in the exam

Halsey vs Rowan is among the most-asked CMA computations: straight earnings, working back to the wage rate or material cost, or explaining which plan suits a situation. Turnover rates are a dependable MCQ and short numerical. The profit foregone from turnover (Ill. 15) is a favourite 5-mark question.

## Wage systems

- **Time rate:** wages = time worked (including normal idle time) × rate. Suits work that cannot be measured (helpers, supervisors, clerks), highly skilled work, and output paced by the machine (automatic chemical plants).
- **Piece rate:** wages = units × rate per unit; independent of time.
- **Premium bonus:** a standard time is set; the worker gets the time wage up to standard (guaranteed, except under Barth); time saved is **shared** between worker and employer.
- Also: group bonus schemes; incentives for indirect workers.

## Halsey plan (50% sharing)

**Earnings = time taken × rate + 50% × time saved × rate.**

Advantages: time rate guaranteed with a chance to earn more; equitable, as the employer gains from better methods. Disadvantages: weaker incentive than piece rate (the harder he works, the less per piece); sharing may be disliked.

**ICAI Illustration 6:** rate ₹60, allowed 8 h, taken 6 h, saved 2 h. Earnings = 6 × 60 + ½ × 2 × 60 = 360 + 60 = **₹420**.

## Rowan plan (variable sharing)

**Earnings = time taken × rate + (time saved ÷ time allowed) × time taken × rate.**

Advantages: "fool-proof": a worker can **never double** his earnings even with loose rate-setting; better return than Halsey for **moderate** efficiency; sharing appeals to employers. Disadvantages: a bit complicated; incentive weak when time saved exceeds 50%; sharing not welcomed by workers.

**ICAI Illustration 7:** same data. Earnings = 360 + (2 ÷ 8) × 6 × 60 = 360 + 90 = **₹450**.

## When do Halsey and Rowan pay the same? (ICAI Illustration 9)

Halsey bonus = ½ (SH − AH) R; Rowan bonus = (AH ÷ SH)(SH − AH) R. Equal when AH ÷ SH = ½, i.e. **time taken = 50% of time allowed**.

Time allowed 8 h, rate ₹8:

| Time taken | Saved | Halsey bonus | Rowan bonus | Halsey total | Rowan total | Halsey per hour | Rowan per hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 64 | 64 | 8.00 | 8.00 |
| 7 | 1 | 4 | 7 | 60 | 63 | 8.57 | 9.00 |
| 6 | 2 | 8 | 12 | 56 | 60 | 9.33 | 10.00 |
| 5 | 3 | 12 | 15 | 52 | 55 | 10.40 | 11.00 |
| **4** | **4** | **16** | **16** | **48** | **48** | **12.00** | **12.00** |
| 3 | 5 | 20 | 15 | 44 | 39 | 14.67 | 13.00 |
| 2 | 6 | 24 | 12 | 40 | 28 | 20.00 | 14.00 |
| 1 | 7 | 28 | 7 | 36 | 15 | 36.00 | 15.00 |

Rowan pays more below 50% saving, Halsey more above it. **Rowan's bonus peaks at 50% time saved.**

## Working backwards (ICAI Illustrations 8 and 10)

**Ill. 8:** time allowed 50 h; A (Rowan) takes 30 h, B (Halsey) 40 h; overhead ₹5 per hour worked; factory cost A ₹3,490, B ₹3,600. Let X = material, Y = wage rate.
- A: X + 30Y + (20 ÷ 50) × 30Y + 150 = 3,490 → X + 42Y = 3,340.
- B: X + 40Y + ½ × 10Y + 200 = 3,600 → X + 45Y = 3,400.
- Subtract: 3Y = 60, **Y = ₹20**; **X = ₹2,500**. Check: A = 2,500 + 600 + 240 + 150 = 3,490 ✓; B = 2,500 + 800 + 100 + 200 = 3,600 ✓.

**Ill. 10:** guaranteed ₹30 per hour, standard 4 h; under Rowan the effective rate was ₹37.50. Let T = hours: 37.5T = 30T + ((4 − T) ÷ 4) × 30T → 37.5 = 30 + 7.5(4 − T) → **T = 3 h**. Under Halsey: 3 × 30 + ½ × 1 × 30 = **₹105**; effective rate **₹35**.

## Which plan for a new rate-setting department? (ICAI Illustration 11)

Requirements: gains not all to workers; quality must not suffer; new rate-setters may err. **Rowan** meets all three:
1. Allowed 4 h, taken 3 h, rate ₹5: bonus = (3 ÷ 4) × 1 × 5 = ₹3.75 of the ₹5 saved; employer keeps ₹1.25.
2. Taken 2 h: bonus = (2 ÷ 4) × 2 × 5 = ₹5 (the peak). Taken 1 h: (1 ÷ 4) × 3 × 5 = ₹3.75, so rushing does not pay, and quality is protected.
3. If time allowed is wrongly set at 10 h: bonus = (3 ÷ 10) × 7 × 5 = ₹10.50, about 2 hours' wages for 7 hours saved, so a loose standard is not exploited.

## Efficiency rating and productivity

**Efficiency % = time allowed as per standard ÷ time taken × 100.** Procedure: set standard time (time and motion or work study, with contingency allowances); record actual output and time; compute the rating. Uses: payment by results; manpower budgets.

**ICAI Example 1:** standard hours for budget = 3,480 × 5 + 4,000 × 4 = 33,400; efficiency 80%, so hours needed = 33,400 × 100 ÷ 80 = **41,750**.

**Productivity** = standard time for actual work ÷ actual time taken. Improve by: right skills; right person in the right job; training; avoiding excess or shortage of staff; work study and standardisation.

## Employee turnover

Rate of change in the workforce over a period, against an index (industry or past norm). Average employees on roll = (opening + closing) ÷ 2.

| Method | Formula (× 100) |
|---|---|
| Replacement | Replacements ÷ average employees (new hires for expansion excluded) |
| Separation | Separations ÷ average employees |
| Flux | (Separations + replacements) ÷ average employees |
| Flux with new joinings | (Separations + replacements + new joinings) ÷ average, or (separations + accessions) ÷ average |

Accessions = all joiners, including replacements. **Annualise:** rate for the period × 365 ÷ days in the period (or × 4 for a quarter).

**ICAI Illustration 14:** quarterly rates: flux 10%, replacement 5%, separation 3%; 30 replaced.
1. Average on roll = 30 ÷ 5% = **600**.
2. Separations = 3% × 600 = **18**.
3. Flux (separations + accessions): 10% × 600 = 60 = 18 + A, so accessions = **42** recruited and joined.
4. Annual equivalents: **40%, 20%, 12%**.

**Causes:** personal (better job, ill health or age, domestic reasons, discontent: only discontent is curable); unavoidable (seasonal business, shortage of material or power, slack market, plant relocation, disability, discipline); avoidable (dissatisfaction with job, pay, hours or conditions; strained relations; no training or promotion; no recreation or medical facilities; low wages).

**Effects:** production flow disturbed; new workers less efficient; higher training and induction cost; more tool breakage and material waste; higher recruitment cost.

**Cost of turnover:** **preventive** costs (medical, welfare, pension, retention benefits) and **replacement** costs (recruitment, training, induction, abnormal breakage and scrap, extra wages and overheads from inefficiency). Find the optimum level balancing the two.

## Profit foregone from turnover (ICAI Illustration 15)

Sales ₹83,03,300; P/V ratio 20%; actual hours 4,45,000, including 30,000 training hours, half unproductive; 1,00,000 potentially productive hours lost through delays in filling vacancies.

1. Productive hours = 4,45,000 − 15,000 = 4,30,000.
2. Hours lost = 1,00,000 + 15,000 = 1,15,000.
3. Sales lost = 83,03,300 ÷ 4,30,000 × 1,15,000 = ₹22,20,650.
4. Contribution lost at 20% = **₹4,44,130**.
5. Add settlement 43,820, recruitment 26,740, selection 12,750, training 30,490.
6. **Profit foregone = ₹5,57,930.**

## Traps

- Rowan bonus fraction is saved ÷ **allowed**, multiplied by time **taken** × rate (not time saved × rate).
- Forgetting the guaranteed time wage when time taken exceeds time allowed (no negative bonus).
- Lost contribution, not lost sales: multiply lost sales by the **P/V ratio**.
- Counting expansion hires as replacements.

## What to remember

- Halsey: TT × R + ½ TS × R. Rowan: TT × R + (TS ÷ TA) × TT × R. Equal at 50% saving; Rowan peaks there.
- Ill. 6: ₹420; Ill. 7: ₹450; Ill. 8: rate ₹20, material ₹2,500; Ill. 10: ₹105, ₹35 per hour.
- Turnover: replacement, separation, flux; Ill. 14: average 600, separations 18, accessions 42; annual 40/20/12%.
- Ill. 15: profit foregone ₹5,57,930.

## Flashcards
Q: Halsey earnings formula?
A: Time taken × rate + 50% × time saved × rate.

Q: Rowan earnings formula?
A: Time taken × rate + (time saved ÷ time allowed) × time taken × rate.

Q: When are Halsey and Rowan bonuses equal?
A: When time taken is 50% of time allowed.

Q: Why is Rowan called fool-proof?
A: A worker can never double his earnings, even if the standard time is loosely set.

Q: ICAI Ill. 6 and 7: rate ₹60, allowed 8 h, taken 6 h. Halsey and Rowan earnings?
A: Halsey ₹420; Rowan ₹450.

Q: Flux method formula?
A: (Separations + replacements) ÷ average employees on roll × 100.

Q: ICAI Ill. 14: replacement rate 5%, 30 replaced. Average on roll?
A: 600.

Q: How do you annualise a quarterly turnover rate?
A: Multiply by 4 (or by 365 ÷ days in the period).

Q: Efficiency % formula?
A: Standard time allowed ÷ time taken × 100.

Q: Two types of cost associated with employee turnover?
A: Preventive costs and replacement costs.
