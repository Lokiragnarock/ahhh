# MC-2: Job Costing

Covers: definition (CIMA), principles, process and suitability of job costing; the job cost card; collecting material, labour and overheads for jobs; treatment of spoiled and defective work (three cases); control account entries for jobs; advantages and disadvantages; job vs process costing; with ICAI Illustrations 1-2. **(ICAI SM ch. 9)**

## Where this fits in the exam

A job cost sheet with departmental overhead rates worked from a P&L (Ill. 2 type) is the classic question; spoilage journal entries (Ill. 1) and job vs process costing are frequent MCQs and short theory.

## Meaning and suitability

CIMA: job costing applies "where the work consists of separate contracts, jobs or batches, each of which is authorised by specific order or contract." Each job is a separate cost unit. Industries: printing, furniture, hardware, ship-building, heavy machinery, interior decoration, repairs.

**Principles (purpose):** ascertain the cost of each unit; control cost; determine profitability.

**Process:** a separate cost sheet per job; material issued (from requisitions or bill of materials) and employee cost (from time cards) entered; overheads added on completion.

**Suitable when:** jobs are to customer specification; no two orders are alike; WIP varies with the jobs in hand.

## Job cost card

Records materials issued, hours by employee class, other expenses and the overhead share, with estimated vs actual columns: direct material, direct wages, production overhead → **production cost**; administration, selling and distribution → **total cost**; profit or loss; selling price.

## Collecting costs for a job

- **Material:** separate stores requisitions per job (or the bill of materials itself); priced and entered in a material abstract; posted to job cards in the WIP ledger weekly or monthly. Special material bought for a job is charged **directly** without passing through the stores ledger. Surplus returned to stores on a shop credit note (job credited); used on another job via a material transfer note.
- **Labour:** job time cards; idle time booked to standing orders; labour abstract posted to job cards; indirect labour from payroll to standing orders.
- **Overheads:** collected under standing orders, apportioned, re-apportioned and absorbed (machine hour, labour hour, % of wages or materials). Different bases give different job costs; absorbing overhead into jobs is essentially arbitrary.

## Spoiled and defective work

- **Spoiled work:** totally rejected, cannot be rectified.
- **Defective work:** imperfect but can be rectified with extra cost.

| Case | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Normal (unavoidable) defectives within the normal rate | Rectification charged to the whole job, spread over the batch; excess beyond normal → Costing P&L |
| Defect from **bad workmanship** | Abnormal → Costing P&L (unless recovered from the worker); if management allowed for some, that part is normal and charged to the batch |
| Defect from **inspection wrongly accepting poor material** | Charged to the inspection department; abnormal → Costing P&L |

Inherent defectives may be charged to the specific jobs; if not identifiable with jobs, treat rectification as factory overhead.

### ICAI Illustration 1: spoilage journal

Work order cost ₹1,00,000; 8% spoiled; realisable value ₹2,000; normal spoilage 2%.

- Actual loss ₹8,000; normal ₹2,000; abnormal ₹6,000.
- Realisable value ₹2,000 split 2:6 → normal ₹500, abnormal ₹1,500.

| Dr | ₹ | Cr | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material Control (scrap value) | 2,000 | WIP Control | 8,000 |
| Overhead Control (normal loss 2,000 − 500) | 1,500 | | |
| Costing P&L (abnormal 6,000 − 1,500) | 4,500 | | |

## Control account entries for jobs (ICAI)

Purchases: Stores Control Dr, Cost Ledger Control Cr. Direct materials to jobs: WIP Dr, Stores Cr. Returns from jobs: Stores Dr, WIP Cr. Returns to suppliers: Cost Ledger Control Dr, Stores Cr. Indirect materials: Factory OH Dr, Stores Cr. Wages paid: Wages Control Dr, Cost Ledger Control Cr. Direct wages: WIP Dr, Wages Cr. Indirect wages: Factory OH Dr, Wages Cr. Indirect expenses: Factory OH Dr, Cost Ledger Control Cr. Overhead to jobs: WIP Dr, Factory OH Cr. **Completed jobs: Cost of Sales Dr, WIP Cr.** Cost of sales to Costing P&L; sales value: Cost Ledger Control Dr, Costing P&L Cr.

## ICAI Illustration 2: revising a job price with departmental rates

Supervisor's estimate for Job 303: material ₹70; wages 18 hours at ₹2.50 = ₹45 (X 8 h, Y 6 h, Z 4 h); chargeable expenses ₹5; plus 33⅓% for expenses = ₹160. From the year's P&L: wages X 10,000, Y 12,000, Z 8,000; overheads X 5,000, Y 9,000, Z 2,000; works cost ₹2,00,000; selling expenses ₹20,000.

| Job cost sheet | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Direct materials | 70.00 |
| Direct wages (X 20, Y 15, Z 10) | 45.00 |
| Chargeable expenses | 5.00 |
| Prime cost | 120.00 |
| OH X: 5,000 ÷ 10,000 = 50% of 20 | 10.00 |
| OH Y: 9,000 ÷ 12,000 = 75% of 15 | 11.25 |
| OH Z: 2,000 ÷ 8,000 = 25% of 10 | 2.50 |
| **Works cost** | **143.75** |
| Selling: 20,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = 10% of works cost | 14.38 |
| **Total cost** | **158.13** |
| Profit 20% of cost | 31.63 |
| **Selling price** | **189.76** |

Lesson: a single blanket 33⅓% on prime cost ignores that departments X, Y, Z carry very different overhead rates; departmental rates based on wages (the hourly rates are the same) give the realistic cost.

## Advantages and disadvantages

**Advantages:** cost details for each job available for control; profitability per job; helps production planning; budgetary control and standard costing can be applied; spoilage and defectives identified and responsibility fixed. **Disadvantages:** costly and laborious; more clerical errors; not suitable under inflation; past records lose meaning when market conditions change.

## Job vs process costing

| Job costing | Process costing |
|---|---|
| Specific orders | Continuous flow, homogeneous output |
| Cost per job | Cost per process per period |
| Each job separate | Units lose identity |
| Costs collected against a job number | Unit cost is an average for the period |
| Cost known when the job is complete | Cost calculated at the end of the period |
| More managerial attention needed | Standardised process, easier control |

## Traps

- Splitting the scrap value of spoilage: share it between normal and abnormal in the ratio of the losses (2:6).
- Using one blanket overhead percentage when the question gives departmental figures.
- Completed jobs go to **cost of sales** (job order), not to finished goods stock, in ICAI's job entries.

## What to remember

- Job costing: specific orders, cost per job, job cost card.
- Spoilage Ill. 1: Material Dr 2,000; Overhead Dr 1,500; Costing P&L Dr 4,500; WIP Cr 8,000.
- Ill. 2: works cost ₹143.75; total ₹158.13; price ₹189.76.
- Defects: normal → job; bad workmanship → abnormal (or recover from worker); inspection error → inspection department, abnormal.

## Flashcards
Q: CIMA definition of job costing?
A: The basic costing method applicable where work consists of separate contracts, jobs or batches, each authorised by a specific order or contract.

Q: Difference between spoiled and defective work?
A: Spoiled work is totally rejected and cannot be rectified; defective work can be rectified at extra cost.

Q: Treatment of defects caused by inspection wrongly accepting poor material?
A: Charged to the inspection department as an abnormal cost, to Costing P&L.

Q: ICAI Ill. 1: work order ₹1,00,000, 8% spoiled, normal 2%, scrap value ₹2,000. Amount to Costing P&L?
A: ₹4,500.

Q: How is special material bought for one job recorded?
A: Charged directly to the job, without passing through the stores ledger.

Q: Entry for the total cost of jobs completed?
A: Cost of Sales A/c Dr; To WIP Control A/c.

Q: ICAI Ill. 2: overhead rate for department Y?
A: 75% of direct wages (₹9,000 ÷ ₹12,000).

Q: Two disadvantages of job costing?
A: Any two of: costly and laborious; error-prone clerical work; unsuitable under inflation; past records lose meaning when markets change.
