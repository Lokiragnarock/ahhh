# EL-5: Employee Cost, Payroll, Idle Time and Overtime

Covers: what employee cost includes; direct vs indirect; employee cost control and the departments involved; time-keeping vs time-booking; payroll procedure and deductions; normal and abnormal idle time; overtime (Factories Act rule and the four treatments of overtime premium); the effective hourly rate for costing, holiday and leave wages, night-shift allowance; with ICAI Illustrations 1-5, 12 and 13. **(ICAI SM ch. 3)**

## Where this fits in the exam

The effective hourly rate (including employer PF, ESI, bonus, leave) and the treatment of overtime premium are the usual 4-5 mark computations and MCQs. Idle time treatment is a frequent theory part.

## Employee cost

Benefits paid or payable to permanent or temporary employees, in cash or kind: wages and salary; allowances and incentives; overtime; **employer's** PF and welfare contributions; other benefits (leave with pay, subsidised food, LTC).

| Direct employee cost | Indirect employee cost |
|---|---|
| Workers directly engaged in production | Not directly engaged |
| Identified with the cost unit | Apportioned on a suitable basis |
| Varies with volume | May not vary with volume |

## Employee cost control

Control means keeping **wages per unit of output** low, not the wage bill or each wage. Departments: personnel (recruitment, training, records, appraisal); engineering and work study (job specifications, time and motion study, job analysis and evaluation); time-keeping (attendance and time booking); payroll (pay sheets, disbursement); cost accounting (accumulate, classify, allocate).

**Eight control factors:** manpower assessment; control of time-keeping and booking; time and motion study; control of idle time and overtime; control of turnover; wage and incentive systems; job evaluation and merit rating; productivity.

The cost department splits total wages into: direct cost of jobs; indirect cost (overheads); idle time cost; abnormal loss or gain to P&L.

## Time-keeping vs time-booking

- **Time-keeping** = total time in the factory. Objectives: payroll, overtime, cost control, idle time, discipline, overhead distribution. Methods: manual (attendance register, metal disc or token) and mechanical (punch card, biometric). Requisites: no proxies; record piece-workers too; record arrival and departure; mechanical where possible; record late arrivals; simple and quick; reviewed periodically.
- **Time-booking** = time on each job, for costing, efficiency measurement (actual vs standard) and fixing responsibility. Recorded on job cards (one card per job, many workers) or time cards (one card per worker, all jobs; reconciles easily with attendance). Job time + idle time must equal attendance time.

## Payroll procedure

1. Time-keeping sends days, hours or units (payroll adds overtime and bonus from time-booking).
2. HR sends the list of employees and rates (no bogus employees).
3. Payroll computes wages and pay slips.
4. Accounts deduct statutory items and pay.
5. Statutory deductions plus employer contributions are deposited.

**Deductions:** statutory: employee PF, employee ESI, TDS on salary, professional tax (state). Other: voluntary PF, benevolent fund, loan instalments, advances.

## Idle time

Idle time = time paid − time booked.

| | Causes | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | Walking from gate to workplace, interval between jobs, machine set-up, rest and lunch | Part of cost of production: for direct workers, built into standard hours or the rate; for indirect workers, into the overhead rate |
| **Abnormal** | Poor co-ordination, power failure, breakdown, material shortage, strikes, lockouts, poor supervision, fire, flood | Not in production cost; shown separately in the **Costing P&L Account**, split into controllable (could have been avoided by alert management) and uncontrollable (breakdown, flood) |

### ICAI Illustration 1: effective hourly cost

Basic ₹10,000 pm; DA ₹2,000; bonus 20% of (basic + DA) = ₹2,400; other allowances ₹2,500; employer PF 10% of (basic + DA) = ₹1,200. Monthly ₹18,100, annual **₹2,17,200**. Hours 2,400 − normal idle 400 = 2,000. Effective hourly cost = **₹108.60**.

### ICAI Illustration 2: allocating a week's wages

Six days of 8 hours, less 30 minutes meals and 10 minutes rest = 7 h 20 m a day, so 44 hours a week. Wages (₹100 + 120% DA) × 6 = ₹1,320; rate ₹30 per hour. Booked: X 15, Y 12, Z 13 = 40 hours; 4 hours waiting for work = abnormal idle.

Allocation: X ₹450, Y ₹360, Z ₹390, **Costing P&L ₹120**.

## Overtime

Overtime payment = normal wages for the overtime hours + **overtime premium** (the extra). **Factories Act 1948:** more than 9 hours in a day or 48 hours in a week earns **twice the ordinary rate**. Ordinary rate = basic plus allowances (including the cash value of concessional food grains), **excluding bonus and overtime wages**. Occasional overtime is healthy (full use of capacity); persistent overtime suggests too little capacity or workers deferring work.

| Cause | Treatment of overtime premium |
|---|---|
| Customer's request (urgency) | Charge to the **job** |
| General production programme or urgent orders | **Overhead** of the department that works overtime |
| Fault of another department | Charge to **that department** |
| Abnormal conditions (flood, earthquake) | **Costing P&L Account** |

### ICAI Illustration 5: the three treatments with numbers

Basic ₹100 per hour; overtime before and after hours 175%, Sundays and holidays 225%. Last year: normal 1,00,000 h, weekday overtime 20,000 h, Sunday overtime 5,000 h. Job Z: 1,000 normal, 100 weekday overtime, 25 Sunday overtime = 1,125 h.

- Average inflated rate = (1,00,00,000 + 35,00,000 + 11,25,000) ÷ 1,25,000 = 1,46,25,000 ÷ 1,25,000 = **₹117**.
- **(a) Regular overtime as policy (worker shortage):** premium is part of employee cost; charge at the inflated rate: 1,125 × 117 = **₹1,31,625**.
- **(b) Irregular overtime for production needs:** job at basic 1,125 × 100 = **₹1,12,500**; premium 100 × 75 + 25 × 125 = **₹10,625 to factory overheads**.
- **(c) At the customer's request:** job ₹1,12,500 + premium ₹10,625 = **₹1,23,125**.

### ICAI Illustration 3: earnings and allocation (summary)

A: basic ₹10,000, DA 50%, 10 overtime hours at double (basic + DA) ÷ 200 hours = 2 × 75 × 10 = **₹1,500**. Employer PF 8% and ESI 2% of basic are added to cost (A: 15,000 + 800 + 200 = ₹16,000; B: 24,000 + 1,280 + 320 = ₹25,600), then split over jobs in the time ratio; overtime charged to job Y where it was worked. Total employee cost ₹43,100 (X 19,200, Y 11,420, Z 12,480).

### ICAI Illustration 4: overtime only beyond 9 hours (key points)

DA ₹968 a month ÷ 176 hours = ₹5.50 per hour for all workers. Only hours beyond 9 a day are overtime at double rate (a 10.5-hour day: 8 normal + 1 extra at normal rate + 1.5 overtime = 3 equivalent hours, 12 in all). Saturday: 4 hours' work paid as 8 with nothing else. Results: A 54 equivalent hours × ₹30.50 = **₹1,647**; B 48 × ₹18 = **₹864**; C 62 × ₹43 = **₹2,666**.

## Wage rate for costing purposes

For direct workers, either treat extra payments as overheads, or **inflate the costing rate**: (total wages + all employer extras) ÷ **effective hours** (after leave and normal idle time). This rate is only for costing, not for paying workers.

**Monetary benefits:** basic, DA, overtime, special allowances, production bonus, employer PF and ESI, pension, leave pay. **Non-monetary:** medical, education and training, recreation, housing and welfare, subsidised canteen. Bonus Act: minimum 8.33% of wages or ₹100 (whichever is greater), up to 20%.

### ICAI Illustration 12

Basic ₹10,000 + DA ₹2,000 pm → ₹1,44,000 a year. Employer PF 10% = ₹14,400; employer ESI (6.5% − 1.75%) = 4.75% = ₹6,840; bonus 2 months = ₹24,000. Total **₹1,89,240**. Effective hours (300 − 15 leave days) × 8 = 2,280. Rate = **₹83 per hour**.

### ICAI Illustration 13

Basic ₹10,000 + DA ₹3,000 + fringe ₹1,000 pm = ₹1,68,000 a year. Days 300 − 20 paid holidays = 280; × 8 = 2,240 hours. Rate = **₹75 per hour**.

**Holiday and leave wages:** either departmental overhead (record separately in the payroll) or included in an inflated costing rate (direct workers only). **Night-shift allowance:** not for any particular job, so **overhead**.

## Traps

- Deducting **employee** PF or ESI from cost. Employee contributions are deductions from pay; only **employer** contributions are an extra cost.
- Dividing annual cost by total hours. Divide by **effective** hours (after leave and normal idle time).
- Charging every overtime premium to the job. Only if the customer asked for it.
- Calling idle time waiting for work "normal". In ICAI Ill. 2 it is abnormal, to Costing P&L.

## What to remember

- Effective rate = all employer costs ÷ effective hours. Ill. 1: ₹108.60. Ill. 12: ₹83. Ill. 13: ₹75.
- Overtime premium: customer → job; general → departmental overhead; another department's fault → that department; abnormal → Costing P&L.
- Ill. 5: inflated rate ₹117; job Z ₹1,31,625 / ₹1,12,500 (+ ₹10,625 overhead) / ₹1,23,125.
- Factories Act: over 9 hours a day or 48 a week → double the ordinary rate (excluding bonus).

## Flashcards
Q: Difference between time-keeping and time-booking?
A: Time-keeping records total time in the factory; time-booking records time spent on each job.

Q: Treatment of normal idle time of direct workers?
A: Part of production cost, built into standard hours or the wage rate.

Q: Treatment of abnormal idle time?
A: Excluded from production cost and shown separately in the Costing P&L Account, analysed into controllable and uncontrollable.

Q: Factories Act overtime rule?
A: Over 9 hours a day or 48 hours a week: twice the ordinary rate of wages.

Q: Does the ordinary rate for overtime include bonus?
A: No. It is basic plus allowances, excluding bonus and overtime wages.

Q: Overtime worked at the customer's request: where does the premium go?
A: Charged to that job.

Q: Overtime worked regularly as policy because of worker shortage?
A: The premium is part of employee cost; jobs are charged at an inflated average rate.

Q: ICAI Ill. 12: total annual cost ₹1,89,240, effective hours 2,280. Costing rate?
A: ₹83 per hour.

Q: Treatment of night-shift allowance?
A: Overhead.

Q: Name the four statutory deductions from payroll.
A: Employee PF, employee ESI, TDS on salary, professional tax.
