# CC-1: Standard Costing and Material Variances

Covers: definitions (CIMA); why standard costing is used; the four types of standards; the five-step process; setting physical and price standards for material, labour and overheads (and the problems); controllable vs uncontrollable and favourable vs adverse; the classification of cost variances; and material cost, price, usage, mix and yield variances with ICAI Illustrations 1-4. **(ICAI SM ch. 13, paras 1-7.1)** Background: [[Management Accounting Unit 4 - SC-1 Standard Costing and Material Variances]] uses the same formulas.

## Where this fits in the exam

Standard costing is one of the most heavily tested CMA topics (Unit 4 is 25-30%). Material variances with mix and yield, with stock or purchase complications (Ill. 4), are standard 5-10 mark questions. Types of standards and the process are theory parts.

## Definitions

- **Standard costing (CIMA):** "Control technique that reports variances by comparing actual costs to pre-set standards so facilitating action through management by exception."
- **Standard cost (CIMA):** "the planned unit cost of the product, component or service produced in a period ... main use in performance measurement, control, stock valuation and the establishment of selling prices."

**Why used:** predicts future cost for decisions; gives targets; used in budgeting and performance evaluation (managers do not trade quality for quantity; variances traced to responsibility); interim profit and inventory valuation.

## Four types of standards

| Type | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Ideal** | Best prices, best equipment, maximum efficiency | Criticised: unattainable, so not taken seriously; variances do not show what was avoidable; no logical disposal of variances |
| **Normal** | Achievable under normal conditions; normal activity = standard hours meeting average sales demand over years | Hard to set (needs forecasting) |
| **Basic (bogey)** | Fixed base-year standards, seldom revised | No variances; actual cost expressed as % of basic cost and compared with current cost %; suits few products with long runs |
| **Current** | Management's expectation for the current period | Variances show efficiency, price and volume differences |

ICAI's view (criticisms section): the most effective standard for control is **attainable good performance**.

## Process (five steps)

1. Set standards. 2. Ascertain actual costs (books, invoices, wage sheets). 3. Compare actual with standard (variances). 4. Investigate reasons and evaluate performance. 5. Dispose of variances (to Costing P&L as per the accounting plan).

## Setting standards

Standards are set in **quantity** (units, hours) and **price** (rate) for material, labour and overheads, using historical data, the production plan and expected future conditions.

**Physical standards** (industrial engineering): consider the operating plan, output, material specification and mix, production method, worker skills, working conditions, external factors (labour law, Factories Act).
- **Material quantity:** standardisation of products → product study → specification list (including expected wastage) → test runs.
- **Labour time:** product study → labour specification → standardised methods → manufacturing layout → time and motion study (learning effect) → training and trial.
- **Overheads:** variable OH usually on labour hours; fixed OH on budgeted volume.

**Problems:** new products with inexperienced workers; new technology; product diversification (new tools); materials not available as specified; choosing the level (best operators 2 h, attainable good performance 2.10 h, past average 2.60 h: maximum efficiency gives low cost but large adverse variances; attainable performance gives near-nil variances); similar choices for waste standards.

**Price standards:** average price expected next year, or normal price over a cycle of seasons. Material price: consider stocks held and their prices, prices on orders already placed, minimum support price, expected fluctuations. Month-to-month fluctuation → year's average adjusted for trend; seasonal with one annual purchase → weighted average of likely prices; year-to-year → statistical estimate. **Wage rate:** time per unit, industry rates, union agreement, law (minimum wages, bonus). **Overheads:** budgeted output (practical capacity and expected demand), fixed and variable budgets; flexible budget if output fluctuates.

## Variance terms

- **Controllable** (preventable by the responsibility centre acting prudently) vs **uncontrollable** (beyond its control; persistent large ones call for revising the standard).
- **Favourable (F):** cost variance where actual cost < standard. **Adverse (A):** actual > standard. (Reversed for sales.)

**Classification:** total cost variance = material (price; usage = mix + yield) + labour (rate; efficiency = mix + yield; idle time) + overhead (variable: expenditure, efficiency; fixed: expenditure, volume = efficiency + capacity + calendar).

## Material variances (ICAI formulas)

| Variance | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Cost (MCV)** | (SQ × SP) − (AQ × AP): standard cost of actual output − actual cost |
| **Price (MPV)** | AQ × (SP − AP), where AQ = quantity **purchased** (or consumed if purchases are not given) |
| **Usage (MUV)** | SP × (SQ − AQ), AQ = quantity **used** |
| **Mix** | SP × (RSQ − AQ) |
| **Yield (sub-usage)** | SP × (SQ − RSQ) |

SQ = standard quantity for actual output; RSQ = actual total input in standard proportion. **Checks:** MCV = MPV + MUV (when purchases = consumption); MUV = mix + yield.

**Responsibility:** price → purchase department (controllable causes); usage → production department. Price variance on purchases reports it **as early as possible**.

### ICAI Illustration 1

Standard 50 units at ₹1; actual 45 at ₹0.80. MPV = 45 × 0.20 = **₹9 F**; MUV = 1 × 5 = **₹5 F**; MCV = 50 − 36 = **₹14 F**.

### ICAI Illustration 2

100 kg of material for 70 kg of output at ₹1; actual output 2,10,000 kg using 2,80,000 kg costing ₹2,52,000 (₹0.90). SQ = 2,10,000 × 100 ÷ 70 = 3,00,000 kg.
MUV = (3,00,000 − 2,80,000) × 1 = **₹20,000 F**; MPV = 0.10 × 2,80,000 = **₹28,000 F**; MCV = 3,00,000 − 2,52,000 = **₹48,000 F**.

### ICAI Illustration 3 (with standard loss)

Standard: 40% A at ₹20, 60% B at ₹30; loss 10% of input. Actual: A 90 kg at ₹18, B 110 kg at ₹34; output 182 kg.

- Standard for 180 kg output: A 80 kg, B 120 kg, ₹5,200. Standard cost of 182 kg = 5,200 × 182 ÷ 180 = ₹5,257.78. Actual ₹5,360.
- **MCV = ₹102.22 A.**
- MPV: A (20 − 18) × 90 = 180 F; B (30 − 34) × 110 = 440 A → **₹260 A**.
- MUV: A (80 × 182/180 − 90) × 20 = 182.22 A; B (120 × 182/180 − 110) × 30 = 340 F → **₹157.78 F**.
- Check: −260 + 157.78 = −102.22 ✓.

### ICAI Illustration 4 (stocks, mix and yield)

Standard mix A 40% at ₹4, B 60% at ₹3; loss 15%; output 1,700 kg. Stocks (opening at standard price): A opening 35, purchases 800 for ₹3,400 (₹4.25), closing 5; B opening 40, purchases 1,200 for ₹3,000 (₹2.50), closing 50.

1. SQ = 1,700 ÷ 0.85 = 2,000 kg: A 800, B 1,200; standard cost ₹6,800.
2. AQ used: A 35 + 800 − 5 = **830**; B 40 + 1,200 − 50 = **1,190**; total 2,020. Actual cost = A (35 × 4 + 795 × 4.25) 3,518.75 + B (40 × 3 + 1,150 × 2.50) 2,995 = ₹6,513.75.
3. RSQ = 2,020 in 40:60 = A 808, B 1,212.

| Variance | A | B | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price (only on purchased units used: 795, 1,150) | 795 × (4 − 4.25) = 198.75 A | 1,150 × 0.50 = 575 F | **376.25 F** |
| Usage (SQ − AQ) × SP | (800 − 830) × 4 = 120 A | (1,200 − 1,190) × 3 = 30 F | **90 A** |
| Mix (RSQ − AQ) × SP | 88 A | 66 F | **22 A** |
| Yield (SQ − RSQ) × SP | 32 A | 36 A | **68 A** |
| **Cost** (6,800 − 6,513.75) | | | **286.25 F** |

Checks: 376.25 F − 90 A = 286.25 F ✓; 22 A + 68 A = 90 A ✓. The opening stock was already at standard price, so it carries **no** price variance.

## Traps

- Price variance on quantity used when purchases are given: ICAI prefers **purchased** quantity (earlier reporting).
- SQ must be for **actual output**, grossed up for standard loss (÷ 0.85, × 182/180).
- RSQ uses **standard** proportions on **actual** total input.
- Basic standards produce no variances; actual is compared as a % of basic.

## What to remember

- MCV = MPV + MUV; MUV = mix + yield.
- Ill. 2: MUV ₹20,000 F, MPV ₹28,000 F, MCV ₹48,000 F. Ill. 3: MCV ₹102.22 A = MPV ₹260 A + MUV ₹157.78 F.
- Ill. 4: MPV 376.25 F, MUV 90 A (mix 22 A, yield 68 A), MCV 286.25 F.
- Most effective standard: attainable good performance.

## Flashcards
Q: CIMA definition of standard costing?
A: A control technique that reports variances by comparing actual costs to pre-set standards, facilitating management by exception.

Q: Three criticisms of ideal standards?
A: Unattainable so not taken seriously; variances do not show avoidable amounts; no logical way of disposing of variances.

Q: How are basic (bogey) standards used without variances?
A: Actual and current costs are each expressed as a percentage of basic cost and compared, including trends over periods.

Q: Material price variance formula and which quantity?
A: AQ × (SP − AP), using quantity purchased (or consumed if purchases are not given).

Q: Material mix variance formula?
A: SP × (RSQ − AQ).

Q: Material yield variance formula?
A: SP × (SQ − RSQ).

Q: ICAI Ill. 2: 100 kg input per 70 kg output; output 2,10,000 kg. Standard quantity?
A: 3,00,000 kg.

Q: ICAI Ill. 4: material usage, mix and yield variances?
A: Usage ₹90 A; mix ₹22 A; yield ₹68 A.

Q: Who is responsible for material usage variance?
A: The production department.

Q: Which type of standard is most effective for control according to ICAI?
A: One representing attainable good performance.
