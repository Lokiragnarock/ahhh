## Hedges resolved

**Leave encashment ceiling, non-government employees (10(10AA)(ii))** — the note's "verify the current figure" hedge:
the ceiling is **₹25,00,000**, not the older ₹3,00,000. Raised by CBDT Notification No. 31/2023 (24 May 2023),
effective retrospectively from 1 April 2023, and still current for AY 2026-27. If received from more than one
employer in the same year, or across multiple years, the ₹25L cap is cumulative/aggregate, not per employer or
per year.
Source: [PIB press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1927265&reg=48&lang=2)

**Partial integration of agricultural income — does it apply under the new regime?**
No. Partial integration is a rate-mechanism that needs a graduated-slab structure to bite; commentators
consistently treat it as inapplicable under Sec 115BAC — agricultural income stays exempt under 10(1) and
simply has no effect on the tax computed on non-agricultural income at new-regime slab rates. Net agricultural
income is still disclosed (Schedule EI in the ITR) but there is no step-1/step-2 rate calculation to perform.
This is well-supported secondary-source consensus rather than a bare statutory clause, since the Act itself
doesn't carry an explicit "no partial integration under 115BAC" sentence — treat it as reliable but not
word-for-word citable to a section number in an answer.
Sources: [Tax Garden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/agricultural-income-tax-india-section-10-1-partial-integration-ay-2026-27), [1Finance](https://1finance.co.in/blog/is-agricultural-income-fully-exempt-from-income-tax/)

**Surcharge under the NEW regime — correcting the student's own `Income Slab.md`.**
That note's surcharge table (10% / 15% / 25% / **37%** topping out above ₹5Cr) is not the 115BAC schedule.
Under the new regime, surcharge is capped lower:

| Total income | New-regime surcharge |
|---|---|
| ₹50L – ₹1Cr | 10% |
| ₹1Cr – ₹2Cr | 15% |
| Above ₹2Cr | 25% |

**There is no 37% surcharge rate under the new regime** — the top rate was cut to a flat 25% maximum when
115BAC became the default, precisely to cap effective tax rates. Additionally, for **capital gains and
dividend income**, surcharge is capped at **15%** regardless of total income. Marginal relief applies at each
surcharge threshold.
Sources: [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/what-is-surcharge-on-income-tax), [CA Rajput](https://carajput.com/blog/differences-in-surcharge-rates-between-new-old-tax-regime/)

**Which Sec 10(14) allowances survive 115BAC?**
Confirmed list — Rule 2BB was amended to preserve exemption under the new regime for: transport allowance
for blind/deaf-dumb/orthopedically-handicapped employees (₹3,200/month), conveyance allowance for official
duty, travelling/transfer allowance, and daily allowance while on tour/transfer away from normal duty station.
Everything else in Rule 2BB's table (children education, hostel, uniform, helper, research/academic, tribal/
border/remote-area allowances) is fully taxable. This matches what `Unit 2 - Salary Chargeability and
Allowances.md` already has — that list was correct, just hedged unnecessarily.
Source: [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/exemptions-available-for-section-115bac-regarding-allowances-perquisites)

**Does the free-food/meal-voucher exemption survive 115BAC?**
**No — this is a correction, not just a hedge resolution.** Rule 3(7)(iii) gives nil valuation for food/
non-alcoholic beverages during working hours (canteen or non-transferable vouchers) up to ₹50/meal — but a
second proviso to that rule specifically **disapplies this exemption for any employee who has opted for
Sec 115BAC**. So for a new-regime taxpayer, employer-provided food/meal vouchers are taxable at the
employer's actual cost, not merely the excess over ₹50/meal — the exemption doesn't exist at all in the
new-regime computation. Contrast with **perquisites generally**, which the cheat sheet correctly notes are
"fully taxable under the new regime, they were never a relief" — free food is a case where the new regime
denies a specific *valuation* concession, on top of that general point.
(This flips again from 1 April 2026: the draft Income-tax Rules 2026 remove that second proviso entirely and
raise the per-meal figure to ₹200 — irrelevant for this AY, but don't let it contaminate an AY 2026-27 answer.)
Sources: [Pazcare](https://www.pazcare.com/blog/how-hrs-should-structure-food-allowance-in-the-new-tax-regime), [TaxTMI Rule 3(7)(iii)](https://www.taxtmi.com/manuals?id=886)

## Could not verify

- **Exact statutory citation for "partial integration doesn't apply under 115BAC."** The conclusion is
  consistent across every secondary source checked, but none of them quote a specific sub-section saying so
  in as many words — it follows from how the Finance Act's rate schedule is structured rather than from an
  explicit carve-out sentence. Safe to state as fact in an exam answer; don't over-claim a section number for
  it without your professor's confirmation.
- **Whether the Rule 3(7)(iii) free-food denial extends to on-premises canteen meals specifically, or only to
  the paid-voucher route.** Every secondary source paraphrases the second proviso as denying "the exemption"
  wholesale to 115BAC opters, which reads as covering both the canteen case and the voucher case (both sit
  under the same first proviso), but I could not pull the bare rule text to confirm the proviso doesn't
  carve out an exception for canteen-only meals. Treat the "denied entirely" position as the safer exam
  answer, but flag this if your professor draws the distinction differently in class.
- **Whether the Income Tax Act, 2025 (the recodification referenced in `Unit 1 - Tax Fundamentals and
  Statutory Framework.md`) is in force for AY 2026-27 or only from AY 2027-28.** Multiple sources reference
  "Section 202" and "Schedule" numbering from the 2025 Act taking over specific provisions from 1 April 2026,
  which would mean the 1961 Act (and all section numbers used throughout these notes) still governs AY
  2026-27 — but I did not find an authoritative single statement pinning the exact transition date. Use the
  1961 Act section numbers for this exam; don't cite "Income Tax Act 2025" section numbers.
