# Experimental Psychology: Practical I — Reference

Lab-skills and experiment-menu reference for the Practical I paper. Comprehensive exam for this paper is in Semester II (35 hours total); Semester I work is the skills-building and lab-record stage. This is a quick-lookup sheet, not a set of teaching notes — pair it with the GP1 unit notes for the underlying theory behind each technique/experiment.

## Objectives

- Develop practical skills in the psychological domain (translating GP1's theory into hands-on demonstration and measurement).
- Learn the basic psychological processes involved in human behaviour through direct experimentation, not just reading about them.

## A. Professional Skills (Demonstration / Viva)

Students must learn a minimum of **any TWO techniques** in each of the following two areas, and submit a process record for each at the end of Semester I:

- **Attention/Concentration Techniques** — e.g., techniques for measuring or training sustained/selective/divided attention (see GP1 Unit 2's attention section for the underlying theory: selective, divided, and sustained attention, and their determinants).
- **Learning Methods** — e.g., techniques for demonstrating classical/operant conditioning or trial-and-error learning in a controlled demonstration setting (see GP1 Unit 4 for the underlying theory).

## B. Psychological Process Experiments — choose any 8 of the 16 listed

Organised into four process categories, each with a matching GP1 unit for the theory behind it.

### Sensory Process (theory: GP1 Unit 2 — Sensation and Perception)
1. **Color Blindness** — screening/assessment of colour vision deficiency.
2. **Temperature Attitude Scale** — measuring skin temperature-sense adaptation/thresholds.
3. **Visual Acuity** — measuring sharpness/clarity of vision.
4. **Two-Point Limen** — measuring the minimum distance between two touch points on skin that can be perceived as separate (a classic touch-threshold/JND demonstration).
5. **Kinesthetic Sensitivity** — measuring accuracy of the kinesthetic (muscle/joint position) sense.

### Attention (theory: GP1 Unit 2 — Attention section)
6. **Division of Attention** — measuring performance decline when attention is split across two simultaneous tasks.
7. **Distraction of Attention** — measuring how competing/irrelevant stimuli disrupt focused performance.
8. **Span of Attention** — measuring how much information can be attended to/grasped in a single brief exposure.

### Perceptual Process (theory: GP1 Unit 2 — Perception section)
9. **Muller-Lyer Illusion** — classic demonstration of a perceptual illusion caused by misapplied depth/size cues (see GP1 Unit 2's "illusions" discussion).
10. **Size Weight Illusion** — demonstration of how perceived size affects perceived weight of an object independent of its actual mass.
11. **Depth Perception** — measuring/demonstrating monocular and binocular depth cues in judging distance.
12. **Critical Flicker Fusion** — measuring the frequency at which a flickering light appears to be a steady, continuous light — a classic visual-perception threshold measure.

### Learning (theory: GP1 Unit 4 — Learning)
13. **Insight Learning** — demonstrating sudden, non-gradual problem solving (see GP1 Unit 4's discussion of Kohler's insight learning).
14. **Trial and Error Learning** — demonstrating gradual learning through repeated attempts and error elimination (contrast directly with insight learning above).
15. **Transfer of Learning** — demonstrating how learning one task affects (positively or negatively) performance on a related task.
16. **Knowledge of Results on Learning** — demonstrating how feedback about performance accuracy affects the rate/quality of learning.
