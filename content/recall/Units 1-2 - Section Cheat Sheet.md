# Units 1–2 — Section Cheat Sheet

One page. Section number on the left, what it does on the right. Citing the section in a written
answer earns marks on its own, so this is the highest-density thing to hold.

Reasoning behind any of these: [[Units 1-2 - How Income Tax Actually Works]].
**Rupee figures: verify against [[Unit 1-2 Fact Check and Corrections]] before relying on them.**

## Definitions

| Sec | What it defines |
|---|---|
| **2(1A)** | Agricultural income — three limbs: rent/revenue from land **in India**, income from agricultural operations incl. marketing-necessary processing, farm building income |
| **2(7)** | **Assessee** — person liable to tax, interest or penalty, or under proceedings. Broader than taxpayer |
| **2(9)** | **Assessment year** — the 12 months following the PY, in which income is assessed |
| **2(31)** | **Person** — individual, HUF, company, firm, AOP/BOI, local authority, artificial juridical person (7 categories) |
| **3** | **Previous year** — FY in which income is earned. First PY of a new source can be shorter than 12 months, never longer |

## Charge and scope

| Sec | What it does |
|---|---|
| **4** | Charging section — income tax is charged on the total income of the previous year |
| **5** | **Scope of total income** — the ROR / RNOR / NR table. Received, accrued, or deemed either |
| **6(1)** | Resident test — 182 days, **or** 60 days + 365 days over preceding 4 years |
| **6(1)** *proviso* | 60-day limb extends to **182 days** for Indian citizens leaving for employment / crew, and Indian citizens or PIOs **visiting** India |
| **6(1A)** | **Deemed resident** — Indian citizen, Indian-source income above threshold, **not liable to tax in any other country**. Treated as RNOR |
| **6(6)** | ROR vs RNOR — ROR needs **both**: resident in 2 of last 10 PYs **and** 730+ days in last 7 years |
| **7** | **Income deemed to be received** — employer's excess RPF contribution, excess interest credited to RPF, transferred balance from URPF |
| **9(1)(ii)** | **Salary deemed to accrue where services are rendered** — not where paid, not where contracted |
| **9** *generally* | Also deems Indian: income from assets/property in India, capital gains on Indian assets, dividends from an Indian company, interest/royalty/FTS from government or residents |

## Heads and the computation chain

| Sec | What it does |
|---|---|
| **14** | The **five heads** — Salary, House Property, Business/Profession, Capital Gains, Other Sources |
| **45** | Charges **capital gains** — the specific provision that drags a capital receipt into tax. Its existence proves capital receipts are otherwise outside the net |
| **60–65** | **Clubbing** — spouse, son's wife, minor child, revocable transfers. Blocks income-splitting across a family |
| **70–80** | **Set-off and carry-forward** — intra-head, then inter-head, then carry forward. Return must be filed on time to carry a loss forward |
| **87A** | **Rebate** — operates on the *tax* figure, not on income. Why the effective no-tax point sits far above the nil slab |
| **115BAC** | **New tax regime** — now the **default**. Lower rates, almost no reliefs |
| **288A / 288B** | Rounding — **total income** to nearest ₹10 (288A), **tax payable** to nearest ₹10 (288B) |

## Exempt income (Sec 10)

| Sec | Exempts |
|---|---|
| **10(1)** | **Agricultural income** — exempt because agriculture is a **State** subject; the Union has no power to tax it |
| **10(2)** | Sum received by a member out of HUF income |
| **10(2A)** | Partner's share of firm profit — the firm was already taxed |
| **10(10)** | **Gratuity** |
| **10(10A)** | **Commuted pension** |
| **10(10AA)** | **Leave encashment** |
| **10(13A)** | HRA — **switched off by 115BAC**, full HRA taxable |
| **10(14)** | **Prescribed special allowances** — only tour, transfer, daily, conveyance and disabled-transport survive 115BAC |

> Exemption ≠ deduction. **Sec 10 income never enters GTI at all.** Chapter VI-A deductions come off
> *after* income has already been counted into GTI.

## Salary (Unit 2)

| Sec / Rule | What it does |
|---|---|
| **15** | **Chargeability — due or receipt, whichever is earlier.** Anti-avoidance: you can't defer tax by delaying payment. Advance salary taxed on receipt; arrears on receipt if not already taxed |
| **17(1)** | **Definition of salary** — wages, pension, gratuity, fees, commission, perquisites, profit in lieu, advance, leave encashment, annual accretion. Deliberately wide so nothing escapes by relabelling |
| **17(2)** | **Perquisites** — non-cash benefits |
| **17(3)** | **Profit in lieu of salary** — termination compensation, URPF payments, keyman policy sums, amounts before joining / after leaving |
| **Rule 3** | **Perquisite valuation** — the invented-number table (accommodation, car, loan, education, servants, utilities, food, gifts) |
| **16(ia)** | **Standard deduction** — the only Sec 16 deduction 115BAC allows |
| **16(ii)** | Entertainment allowance, government employees — **not available under 115BAC** |
| **16(iii)** | Professional tax — **not available under 115BAC** |
| **89** | **Relief for arrears** — softens the bunching effect when several years' income lands in one |

## The formulas

**Gratuity — least of three (10(10))**
- *Government*: fully exempt, no ceiling
- *Covered by Payment of Gratuity Act*: actual / **(last salary ÷ 26) × 15 × years** (≥6 months rounds up) / statutory ceiling
- *Not covered*: actual / **half month's average salary (last 10 months) × completed years** (no rounding) / ceiling

**Leave encashment — least of four (10(10AA))**
- *Government*: fully exempt
- *Non-government*: actual / 10 months' average salary / cash equivalent of unutilised leave (max 30 days per year of service) / **₹25,00,000** ceiling
- **During service: fully taxable, no relief**

**Commuted pension (10(10A))**
- *Government*: fully exempt
- *Non-government, gratuity also received*: **1/3** of full (100%) commutation value
- *Non-government, no gratuity*: **1/2** of full commutation value
- **Uncommuted (monthly) pension: fully taxable for everyone**

**Partial integration of agricultural income** — *graduated-slab rate mechanism*
1. Tax on (non-agri income + net agri income)
2. Tax on (basic exemption limit + net agri income)
3. **Payable = step 1 − step 2**
- Gates: net agri income above ₹5,000 **and** non-agri income above basic exemption
- **Sources say it does not apply under Sec 115BAC.** But this is secondary-source consensus,
  not a citable sub-section. **If a problem hands you agricultural income and asks you to compute,
  run the two-step anyway and state your assumption** — the marks are for demonstrating the
  mechanism. If asked as theory, say it is a rate device that needs a graduated slab structure to
  bite.

**Salary computation format**
```
Basic + DA + bonus + commission            xxx
+ Allowances (less surviving exemptions)   xxx
+ Perquisites (Rule 3)                     xxx
+ Profit in lieu (17(3))                   xxx
+ Retirement benefits (less 10(10)/(10A)/(10AA))  xxx
─────────────────────────────────
GROSS SALARY                               xxx
Less: Sec 16 (new regime: standard only)  (xxx)
─────────────────────────────────
INCOME FROM SALARY                         xxx
```

**Tax chain**
```
5 heads → ± clubbing → ± set-off → GTI → less Ch VI-A → TOTAL INCOME
→ round to ₹10 → slab rates → less 87A rebate → + surcharge → + cess
→ less TDS/advance tax → PAYABLE or REFUND
```

## Seven traps

0. **Rent-free accommodation is the only city test that fires.** RFA (perquisite, Rule 3) uses **population bands, 10% / 7.5% / 5%** and *adds* to gross salary. Don't reach for the metro / non-metro 50% / 40% split you may have seen attached to HRA — 115BAC gives HRA no exemption at all, so that test never runs in a computation.
1. **Remittance is not receipt.** Income received abroad and later brought to India stays foreign, permanently.
2. **Salary accrues where services are rendered** (9(1)(ii)) — not where paid, not where the contract was signed.
3. **"Salary" means different things in different formulas.** Basic + DA (if retirement-linked) + turnover commission for leave encashment; something narrower for gratuity. Read what each formula asks for.
4. **DA only enters the PF / gratuity / leave encashment bases if it forms part of retirement benefits** under the terms of employment. Stated in the question because it changes the answer.
5. **Perquisites are fully taxable under the new regime.** They were never a relief — they are income being measured.
6. **Commuted pension: the 1/3 or 1/2 is of the FULL (100%) commutation value**, not of the amount actually received for the part commuted.
