### Course Description

India has a well-structured and comprehensive taxation system, operating within a three-tier federal framework comprising the Union Government, State Governments, and Urban/Rural Local Bodies. The authority to levy taxes and duties is distributed among these tiers as per the provisions of the Indian Constitution. For management students, understanding tax laws is crucial as it provides insights into the dynamic tax environment and its implications.

This course introduces students to the fundamental principles of income tax law, focusing on the five major heads of income and their key provisions. It also covers the computation of individuals' tax liabilities under the new tax regime.

### Course Objectives

1. To apply the statutory framework, basic concepts, and legal provisions of income tax to classify income, determine residential status, and assess tax incidence for individuals.
2. To analyse income under the head salary by applying relevant provisions, exemptions, and deductions in accordance with the Income Tax Act.
3. To analyse income from house property and profits and gains from business and profession by applying the provisions, exemptions, and deductions as per Income Tax Act.
4. To analyse capital gains and income from other sources by applying the provisions, exemptions, and deductions as per Income Tax Act.
5. To evaluate individual tax liability under the new tax regime by integrating income from different heads in accordance with income tax provisions.

### Course Learning Outcomes (CLO)

- **CLO 1:** Apply the statutory framework, basic concepts, and legal provisions governing income tax to classify income and assess tax applicability.
- **CLO 2:** Analyse income under the head salary by applying relevant provisions, exemptions, deductions, and valuation rules in accordance with the Income Tax Act.
- **CLO 3:** Analyse income from house property and profits and gains from business and profession by applying the provisions, exemptions, and deductions as per the Income Tax Act.
- **CLO 4:** Analyse capital gains and income from other sources by applying the provisions, exemptions, and deductions as per Income Tax Act.
- **CLO 5:** Evaluate individual tax liability under the new tax regime by integrating income from different heads in accordance with income tax provisions.

## Section II — Syllabus Coverage

### Unit I — Introduction to Taxation
**Weeks:** 6 Jul – 11 Jul, 13 Jul – 18 Jul (2 hrs) · **Hours:** 4 + 2

- Meaning of Taxation and Tax as per the Income Tax Act of 1961 and 2025
- Types of Taxes
- Difference between direct and indirect taxes; Duty, Cess, Surcharge
- Principles of a Good taxation system
- Tax Regimes: Old and New Regime
- Objectives of levying taxes
- Legal enactments governing Tax Administration in India
- Concept of Capital and Revenue receipts and expenses
- Basic Concepts in Taxation: Assessee, Person, Assessment Year, Previous Year, Agricultural Income, Income, GTI, Total Income, Average Rate of Tax
- Determination of residential status (Practical Problems)
- Kinds of Income, and incidence of tax (Practical problems)
- Exempted incomes (Self-study)

Teaching/learning: Socratic Method & AI-based Reflective activities; in-class non-graded Quiz on Kahoot/Curipod; text cases and problem solving on residential status, incidence of tax, computation of total income.
Resources: *Income Tax Law and Practice AY 2026-27*, Gaur & Narang, Kalyani Publication; https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/reckoner

See: [[Unit 1 - Tax Fundamentals and Statutory Framework]], [[Unit 1 - Basic Concepts and Residential Status]], [[Unit 1 - Capital vs Revenue and Exempted Incomes]]

### Unit II — Income from Salary
**Weeks:** 13 Jul – 18 Jul (2 hrs), 20 Jul – 25 Jul, 27 Jul – 2 Aug · **Hours:** 4 + 4 + 2

- Perquisites: rent-free accommodation, concessional accommodation, employer-provided motor car, free or concessional education facility, free domestic servants, gas/electricity/water facility, free or subsidised food and refreshments, medical facilities provided by employer, employer's contribution exceeding prescribed limits to RPF/superannuation fund, interest-free or concessional loans, club membership expenses paid by employer, gifts or vouchers exceeding prescribed limits, free travel facility, employer-paid insurance premium, and any other benefit or amenity provided by the employer
- Treatment of Provident Fund
- Profit in Lieu of Salary
- Leave Encashment (Theory)
- Retirement Benefits: Gratuity / Pension / Commuted pension (Problems)
- Deductions from Gross Salary and Computation of taxable salary (Practical Problems)

Teaching/learning: Socratic Method, AI-based Reflective activities, quick text cases & problem solving; in-class non-graded Quiz on Kahoot/Curipod; text cases and practical problems on HRA, RFA, car perks, pension, gratuity, salary deductions, computation of taxable salary.
Resources: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/income-tax-act-2025

See: [[Unit 2 - Salary Chargeability and Allowances]], [[Unit 2 - Perquisites]], [[Unit 2 - PF Gratuity Pension and Leave Encashment]], [[Unit 2 - Deductions and Computation of Taxable Salary]]

## Section IV — CIA Assessment Details

### CIA-1A — Field Survey with Public and Expert Interview (15 Marks, converted from 20)
**CLO:** CLO 1 · **PCG:** PCG 2 — Functional Knowledge and Application (UG) · **PLO:** 2.1 (RBTL4), 2.2 (RBTL5)

Conduct a field survey and expert interview to understand public/expert perception of the Indian tax system.

- **Part A — Field Survey:** structured questionnaire, minimum 10 respondents across backgrounds (salaried, business, professional, student, self-employed), covering awareness of direct/indirect taxes, income tax slabs, compliance, filing of returns, fairness of rates, effectiveness of the system. Mix objective and open-ended questions; present analysis with tables/charts.
- **Part B — Expert Interview:** two respondents, at least one a Chartered Accountant, businessperson, or working professional. Cover awareness and practical experience of taxation (income categories, tax rates, residential status, compliance, filing). Include comparison with one foreign country's tax system (rates, residential status, taxable income categories). Record as an 8–10 minute video.

**Deliverables:**
1. Video interview recording, 8–10 minutes, clear audio/video, professional conduct.
2. Survey analysis report, 3–4 pages: introduction/objectives, questionnaire design, respondent profile, findings summary/analysis, key insights from interviews, conclusion/recommendations, comparison between expert opinions and survey responses.

**Submission:** Google Classroom, scanned handwritten report + video. **Deadline:** 26/07/2026 (subject to campus-level COE approval). **Type:** Individual.
